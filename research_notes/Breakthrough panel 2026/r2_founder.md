- **I blamed recurrence for run-to-run noise** (5.5% vs 6.3% wrong merges on identical code). The report's own correction names the server: vLLM can answer the same prompt differently depending on what else shares the GPU [checked: test 2 report §16.4, lines 652–654]. Carrying the index forward spreads that noise. It does not create it.
- **I leaned on deferred reference, which test 2 cannot show.** Its world says "Kofi's flatmate" only after the relation was stated [checked by the engineer, confirmed by the moderator]. The 11% was a failed lookup, not a decision made too early.
- **I counted rows, not answers.** Today I traced every wrong hard-set answer of B (the 4B's own index, thinking on) to a cause. I used saved files only, with no model call [checked: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-nichtec/9f081756-7692-5083-b72d-9d96c9efa3d9/scratchpad/panel/founder/r2_gap.py`, output in `r2_gap_out.txt` beside it]. ## 3. Is the convergence real?

Partly. The diagnosis rests on code-checked numbers, but the agreement is not independent and is overstated.

**The strongest argument against it:**
1. **One kind world.** All the evidence comes from a world generated by code from tidy tables, where definitions always come before use. A code binder that undoes the generator looks perfect there; that is what the simulation shows. Real chat has no hidden table.
2. **Half, not the root** (table above).
3. **The field went the other way.** MemReader-4B, a trained phone-size writer, learns by reinforcement to retrieve past context *before* writing, and to defer ambiguous input [checked: arXiv 2604.07877, abstract]. Our evidence that "memory in view while writing" hurts comes only from an untrained, prompted 4B. Five copies of one model may have mistaken "the fix possible without training" for "the root".
4. **Not independent.** Same model, same brief, same moderator draft (A5 already names write-time identity). Three of us separately reached for "remove recurrence, like 2017", and one of us invented a citation for his case. That is a trope, not a finding.

**What survives:** for an untrained phone model on this world, deciding identity while writing causes about half the errors. It also makes the memory impossible to reproduce or re-parse [checked: §16.3]. Worth fixing; not shown to be the root of the owner's problem.

## 5. To the owner

**Is anything here 2017-class? No.** All five of us put it at 1–2%, and I agree. Every part exists, much of it published this year. And 2017 was not first principles. It was one deletion, forced by a real bottleneck, proven on a shared test, by a team with far more compute [checked in round 1: arXiv 1706.03762; the compute point is recall].

**What we learned.** Your phone model can connect things across time: with the right messages and time to think, it scores 95.5%. It fails at two plainer jobs: keeping straight who is who, and finding the right messages cheaply. That is engineering, and it can be fixed.

**The best honest month:** the gates pass, and on the sealed world a phone model with identities kept by code beats today's answers.
- It answers in about 5 seconds instead of 3.5 minutes.
- It makes almost no wrong-person memories.
- Deletion is exact.
- You also get a first number from a real life: yours.

That is a base people could trust, not a new science. More likely: gate 0 passes, the describer lands between the lines, and you learn which single-message errors remain. Then training the describer starts.
