### NAS — Norm-Anchor Scaling (arXiv 2602.02543)
- Title on arXiv/HF: "Toward Ultra-Long-Horizon Sequential Model Editing" (Liu, Zhu, Miao, Fujisawa; ICML-style template). The user-supplied title "Norm Anchors Make Model Edits Last" does NOT match the version indexed on HF (could be a revised title/venue version; unverified). — https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.02543
- Claim: L&E update rules induce exponential growth of edited MLP weight norm under sequential edits; NAS rescales each target value vector v_new to an anchor norm a = E||v_new|| measured by N=1000 pilot edits on the clean model; "one line of code". Proven bounded weight norm. Default "NAS" = AlphaEdit + NAS.
- Setting: atomic sequential editing (1 edit per step, weights committed after every edit) on the FULL CounterFact stream (20,877 edits) and FULL ZsRE stream (19,086 edits in text; 19,082 in Fig.1 caption — internal inconsistency). Models: Llama3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, GPT-J (GPT2-XL in appendix). Single H100.
- Table 1 (end of full stream), CounterFact Eff/Gen/Spe/Flu/Consis; ZsRE Eff/Gen/Spe:
  - Llama3-8B: NAS 97.95/82.71/60.44/619.72/31.88; ZsRE 93.17/88.30/32.15. AlphaEdit 66.27/58.75/49.98/555.21/4.12; ZsRE 22.30/20.34/1.57. RLEdit CF 65.38/49.18/47.86; ZsRE 81.59/78.77/27.03. UltraEdit CF 60.06/52.90/43.88; ZsRE 77.61/75.15/50.26. MEMIT CF 50.95/50.95/49.05; ZsRE 0.95/0.22/0.22. Pre-edited CF Spe 89.79, ZsRE Spe 32.09.
  - Qwen2.5-7B: NAS CF 90.44/55.05/73.21; ZsRE 96.84/88.10/42.52. AlphaEdit CF 84.59/58.41/68.96; ZsRE 97.25/86.17/40.16 (AlphaEdit survives on Qwen). RLEdit ZsRE 93.75/85.37/35.61.
  - GPT-J: NAS CF 98.73/90.68/60.75; ZsRE 92.08/85.04/22.24. AlphaEdit CF 62.65/54.57/54.73; ZsRE 86.13/79.43/21.84. RLEdit CF 78.10/54.86/48.78.
- Note: CounterFact specificity for NAS (~60) is far below the pre-edit model (~84–90) after 20k edits → locality is substantially damaged even for the best method.
- Collapse point (CP@60, CounterFact score ≤60): MEMIT & PRUNE 400→1.5k with NAS; RECT 400→1.4k; AlphaEdit 4k→>21k. GLUE-style capability: prior L&E baselines drop to near zero within ≤4k edits; NAS keeps non-trivial GLUE through 20,877 edits (MRPC noticeably declines).
- Baselines GRACE, MELO, WISE, MEMOIR, ENCORE, LyapLock only in Appendix C.4 (not read).
- Stated limitation: fixed anchor may be suboptimal for non-stationary streams / heterogeneous edit difficulty.
- CounterFact metrics here are teacher-forced probability comparisons (P[new] > P[old]); ZsRE is top-1 token accuracy (teacher-forced) — see the "mirage" critique.

### UltraEdit (arXiv 2505.14679; Gu, Huang, Gu, Zhang; reviewed on OpenReview id GoJLp3BIRV — venue listed as TMLR-style "Reviewed on OpenReview") — https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679
- Method: training-free, "subject-free", memory-free; per turn, hidden state h_i (at answer-label position) + gradient ∇y_i are concatenated, "lifelong normalization" (running mean/var across all turns), then a closed-form RIDGE least-squares update per module: Δθ = (HᵀH + I)⁻¹ HᵀV (Eq. 6–7) solved over the CURRENT TURN's batch only (not over all past edits). Authors frame it as MEMIT-like with whitened covariance.
- Scale: UltraEditBench (Wikidata5M triples, questions generated by GPT-4o-mini) with >2M editing pairs; UltraEdit* evaluated at 100K edits (ZsRE, FEVER), 500K (WikiBigEdit), 2M (UltraEditBench). Baselines only at 20K (ZsRE/FEVER/UltraEditBench) and 17K (WikiBigEdit). 100 edits per turn (WISE: 1 per turn). Metric: Exact Match (token-level, following AlphaEdit/RLEdit), plus WILD LLM-as-judge complementary.
- Table 2 (20K ZsRE edits unless *), Eff/Gen/Spe:
  - GPT-J: UltraEdit ZsRE 78.03/72.42/27.05; UltraEdit* (100K) 72.95/68.68/25.91; RLEdit 73.34/68.93/22.00; AlphaEdit 50.23/43.31/12.54; WISE 34.13/33.14/26.81.
  - Mistral-7B-v0.3: UltraEdit 85.30/80.80/47.38; AlphaEdit 0.00/0.00/0.00 (collapsed); RLEdit 72.57/68.87/23.17.
  - LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct: UltraEdit 90.07/87.36/49.51; UltraEdit* (100K) 87.80/85.48/46.74; RLEdit 91.34/89.68/41.94; AlphaEdit 74.34/67.85/22.94; WISE 40.94/40.27/37.40.
  - WikiBigEdit (17K, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct): UltraEdit Eff/Gen/Spe/Personas/Reasoning 79.60/73.49/48.51/66.55/35.64; UltraEdit* (500K) 68.99/63.59/52.28/55.04/–; RLEdit 75.35/70.00/37.21/65.55/28.13; AlphaEdit 63.24/54.68/20.17/42.58/0.01.
- Table 3 UltraEditBench, UltraEdit* at 2M edits, Eff/Gen/Spe: GPT-J 81.65/76.80/76.44; Mistral-7B 81.70/77.25/77.09; LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct 83.45/79.11/78.05; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 80.70/75.78/76.01. (At 20K: RLEdit LLaMA-3 85.69/81.88/65.64; AlphaEdit LLaMA-3 4.51/3.16/2.78; WISE LLaMA-3 42.27/41.65/39.79.)
- Claims 7x faster than prior SOTA and 4x less VRAM; "only method" to edit a 7B model on 24GB GPU.
- Caveat (inferred): WikiBigEdit multi-hop "Reasoning" stays ~30–36% — sequential edits do not propagate to multi-hop questions. Note that NAS paper (2602.02543) re-ran UltraEdit with 1 edit/step on full CounterFact and found much weaker numbers (Llama3 CF 60.06/52.90/43.88) — results depend heavily on batch-per-turn protocol and metric.

### RLSEdit (arXiv 2601.15686, Jan 2026; Wang, Lyu, Gu, Huang, Lu, Cui, Chang — McGill/Mila) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.15686
- VERY RELEVANT TO NOVELTY CHECK: formulates lifelong editing as ONLINE REGULARIZED LEAST SQUARES over a CUMULATIVE key-value fitting objective (all edits so far) with two regularizers (deviation from pre-trained weights; deviation from an anchor mapping), solved by recursive least squares via Woodbury identity; per-edit cost independent of history length. Provides deviation bounds, asymptotic adherence–preservation characterization. "Soft constraints" vs AlphaEdit "hard preservation" and L&E "hard writes".
- Claims stable scaling to 10K edits on Llama-3 and Qwen2.5; retains early edits; preserves GLUE and held-out reasoning/code benchmarks. (Table numbers not yet read.)
- Difference vs user's method: RLSEdit edits a dense existing MLP weight matrix (in-place), recursive (order-dependent only through regularizer/anchor? exact RLS is order-independent for pure LS), no sparse slot memory, no explicit deletion or capacity certificate (not claimed in abstract).

### EtCon (arXiv 2512.04753, Dec 2025; Li R. et al., Wuhan U./Shanghai Innovation Inst./SJTU) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.04753
- Edit-then-Consolidate: (1) Targeted Proximal SFT (TPSFT) with trust-region objective to limit policy drift; (2) consolidation with GRPO aligning edited knowledge with CoT inference under autoregressive generation. Motivation: gap between teacher-forcing evaluation and real-world lifelong autoregressive effectiveness; overfitting; lack of consolidation stage. (Numbers not yet read.)

### MeG — Massive Editing via Dynamic Weight Generation (ICLR 2026; arXiv 2512.14395; code github.com/RodeWayne/MeG-for-Knowledge-Editing)
- Attaches ONE dynamic-weight neuron to specific layer(s); a diffusion model conditionally generates that neuron's weights from the input query; a "familiarity network" decides whether the query concerns edited knowledge. Pipeline trains: text encoder → familiarity network → neuron weights → weight-generation (diffusion) model — i.e., trained on the full edit set (batch/"massive" editing, compared against MALMEN), NOT an online sequential editor (inferred from README pipeline; confirm). — https://github.com/RodeWayne/MeG-for-Knowledge-Editing
- Reported (search snippet of arXiv v4, not primary-table verified): ZsRE & CounterFact, 1,024→10,000 edits; ZsRE 10,000 edits Score 82.80 on Phi-2 vs MALMEN 45.64; Locality on GPT-J 83.99%. — https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14395v4 [UNVERIFIED: proxy blocks arXiv/OpenReview/ICLR proceedings]

### Mirage of Model Editing (arXiv 2502.11177; Yang W., Sun F., et al., ICT-CAS/Baidu; ACL 2025 per later citations — venue not verified here) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.11177
- Introduces QAEdit (edits derived from NQ/TriviaQA/SimpleQA-style QA datasets) and WILD (Without Intervention, Live Decoding) evaluation.
- Single-edit: current methods average 38.5% success under WILD on QAEdit vs 96.8% reported in literature.
- Four flaws in "synthetic" evaluation: identical edit/test prompts; teacher forcing (feeds ground-truth tokens, leaks content & length); output truncation to target length; match-ratio metric rewarding partial matches. Teacher forcing and target-length truncation cause the largest overestimation.
- Sequential editing under WILD: average success drops to ~10% after only 1,000 edits.
- Evaluated six representative methods (incl. ROME, WISE) on Llama-2-7b-chat and two other LLMs.
- UltraEdit (2505.14679) subsequently adopted WILD as a complementary LLM-as-judge evaluation.

- Repo README (primary): "We audit the MQuAKE benchmark ... and find that 33–76% of its questions and labels are corrupted due to unintentional clerical or procedural oversights." Releases MQuAKE-Remastered + G-Walk ("a simple graph-walk-based approach for multi-hop knowledge editing that does not exploit the quirks of the original dataset").

Four released splits: Remastered-CF-3k (same size as original CF-3k, balanced 1,000 cases per {2,3,4}-hop), Remastered-CF-9k (full size of MQuAKE-CF), Remastered-CF-6334 (train/test splits for parameter-based editors; edit_num ∈ {100, 1000, 3000, 6334}), Remastered-T (temporal).
- [UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET, from ICLR 2025 proceedings PDF via search]: original MQuAKE-CF-3k-v2 has 17 duplicate cases and 633 cases with missing information in instructions; error types named "Inner Contamination" (most impact at high edit intensity, e.g., 3000 edits) and "Intra Contamination" (most impact at low intensity, e.g., 100 edits). On MQuAKE-CF-3k with 1000 edits MeLLo 14.63% vs GWalk 57.37% (model/dataset-version for this pair unverified). — https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f782860c2a5d8f675b0066522b8c2cf2-Paper-Conference.pdf

### CHECK — "Knowledge Editing for Multi-Hop Question Answering Using Semantic Analysis" (IJCAI 2025; Dominic Simon & Rickard Ewetz; arXiv 2508.00914) — https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0916.pdf

### HYPE — Lifelong Model Editing with Graph-Based External Memory (ACL Findings 2025, 2025.findings-acl.690; arXiv 2505.18343; Yash Kumar Atri et al.) — https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.690/

### AlphaEdit (ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.02355v4; Fang, Jiang, Wang, Ma, Shi, Wang, He, Chua — USTC/NUS) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2410.02355
- Projects the MEMIT-style perturbation onto the null space of preserved-knowledge keys K0 (projection P computed once), so outputs on preserved keys are unchanged; the update solves for current edits while "previously updated knowledge remain unaffected" (Eq.14 vs MEMIT Eq.15: Δ_MEMIT = R K1ᵀ (Kp Kpᵀ + K1 K1ᵀ + K0 K0ᵀ)⁻¹, where Kp = keys of previously edited facts). Claims +36.7% avg boost to L&E methods with one extra line.
- Table 1 (sequential editing; per UltraEdit/RLEdit descriptions the protocol is 100 edits per batch; total 2,000 edits per my recollection — total count NOT verified in text read). Eff/Gen/Spe/Flu/Consis (CounterFact) | Eff/Gen/Spe (ZsRE):

  - GPT-J: AlphaEdit 99.75/96.38/75.48/618.50/42.08 | 99.79/96.00/28.29. MEMIT 98.55/95.50/63.64 | 94.91/90.22/30.39.
  - GPT2-XL: AlphaEdit 99.50/93.95/66.39 | 94.81/86.11/25.88.
- Weakness (from later work): collapses in longer streams — NAS paper reports AlphaEdit CP@60 ≈ 4k edits (1 edit/step) on Llama3 and ZsRE Eff 22.30 at 19k edits; UltraEdit reports AlphaEdit 0.00 on Mistral-7B at 20K ZsRE edits and 4.51 Eff on LLaMA-3 UltraEditBench 20K.

### WISE (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2405.14768; Wang P., Li Z., Zhang N., ... Chen H. — ZJU/Alibaba) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2405.14768
- Dual parametric memory: main memory (pretrained FFN) + side memory (copy of an FFN value matrix) for edits; router based on activation score decides which memory; knowledge sharding into random-mask subspaces then Ties-Merge; WISE-Retrieve variant keeps several side memories and routes top-1.
- Table 2 (ZsRE, sequential, one edit per step, T = number of edits; Rel/Gen/Loc/Avg):
  - LLaMA-2-7B: WISE T=1: .98/.92/1.00/.97; T=100: .90/.81/1.00/.90; T=1000: .77/.72/1.00/.83. GRACE T=1000: .93/.08/1.00/.67 (memorizes but does not generalize). MEMIT-MASS T=1000: .69/.65/.62/.65. MEMIT (sequential) T=1000: .04/.04/.02/.03. ROME T=1000 .01/.01/.00.
  - Mistral-7B: WISE T=1000: .70/.67/1.00/.79; GRACE T=1000 1.00/.02/1.00/.67.
- Data: ZsRE T=1,000 (locality from NQ), SelfCheckGPT T=600, Temporal T=100 (GPT-J). Pre-edit ZsRE acc 0.36/0.39 (LLaMA/Mistral).
- Note: WISE evaluated with teacher-forced token accuracy; locality 1.00 comes from router; the "impossible triangle" framing. Later work (UltraEdit Table 2/3) shows WISE at 20K ZsRE edits on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct only 40.94/40.27/37.40.

### RLSEdit details (primary, https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.15686)
- Objective (Eq.15): W*_t = argmin_W Σ_{i=1..t} ||K_i W − V_i||²_F + λ²||W − W0||²_F + μ²||K0 W − V0||²_F — i.e., an exact cumulative ridge least-squares over ALL edits so far plus an anchor term on preserved keys K0/V0; solved online via Woodbury (RLS), per-edit cost independent of history. μ→∞ recovers null-space (AlphaEdit-like) hard preservation; λ controls drift. Residual R_t = V_t − K_t W*_{t−1} used in bounds (Theorem 4.1). Asymptotic consistency to a ridge population minimizer (Prop. 4.2).
- Table 1 (CounterFact, 10K edits total, batch size 100, 3 seeds): Llama-3-8B RLSEdit Eff/Gen/Spe/Flu/Consis 89.94/72.84/60.56/615.58/26.27 vs AlphaEdit 66.78/58.27/51.79/489.91/4.59, MEMIT 49.73/49.24/51.54; Qwen2.5-7B RLSEdit 94.45/68.55/73.37/625.74/31.62 vs AlphaEdit 94.10/70.29/75.29 (AlphaEdit slightly better on Qwen Gen/Spe).
- Relevance to novelty check: this is published (Jan 2026) prior art for "exact joint ridge LS over all edits so far" in lifelong editing — but on a DENSE in-place MLP weight with native activations as keys, recursive (Woodbury) rather than periodic full re-solve, and no claims found (in text read) of exact deletion/unlearning, order-independence guarantees, or a capacity/residual certificate. Note that V_i are computed at edit time against the then-current model, so strict order-independence is not guaranteed.

### MQuAKE original + MeLLo (EMNLP 2023; arXiv 2305.14795 v3) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795

  - GPT-J MEND: 11.7/9.6/7.6/6.1 | 38.2/17.4/12.7/4.6

### Other multi-hop methods (method type; numbers where found)
- PokeMQA (ACL 2024; arXiv 2312.15194; github Hengrui-Gu/PokeMQA): retrieval/in-context; decouples decomposition from a trained scope detector (DistilBERT pre-detector + conflict disambiguator) and entity-linking knowledge prompts (ELQ + Wikidata); evaluated with edited-num 1 and 3000. [UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET] LLaMa-2-7B, 1 edited instance, MQuAKE-CF-3K: multi-hop acc 44.13%, hop-wise acc 30.6% vs MeLLo 33.57%/9.9%. — https://github.com/hengrui-gu/pokemqa
- DeepEdit (arXiv 2401.10471): black-box decoding with semantic constraints + depth-first search over reasoning steps; introduced MQuAKE-2002 and MQuAKE-Hard which "resolve knowledge-conflicting annotation mistakes in the popular KE benchmark MQuAKE-3K". Retrieval/decoding-time (no weight edits). Numbers not obtained. — https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10471
- RAE (CIKM 2024; arXiv 2403.19631; github sycny/RAE): retrieval of edited fact chains via mutual-information maximization over an edited KG + pruning; in-context editing. Evaluated on MQuAKE-CF (3,000 edits) and MQuAKE-T with GPT-2, GPT-J, Falcon, Vicuna, Llama2. Numbers not obtained. — https://github.com/sycny/RAE
- KEDKG (AAAI 2025): external dynamic KG; retrieval-based (see above; numbers UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET).
- CHECK (IJCAI 2025): retrieval/in-context with type-checking (see above).
- G-Walk (ICLR 2025, MQuAKE-Remastered): graph walk over the edited KG; retrieval-based.
- HYPE (ACL Findings 2025): hyperbolic GNN-guided parameter editing (weight edits).
- AcE (2510.07896) and MCircKE (2604.05876): neuron/circuit-level weight edits.
- CaKE (arXiv 2503.16356, "Circuit-aware Editing Enables Generalizable Knowledge Learners"): weight editing aimed at multi-hop generalization (not read).

### GMeLLo (EMNLP 2024 Findings; arXiv 2408.15903) — https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.844/
- KG + LLM: converts edits into a KG, LLM translates question into relation chain/KG query, fuses with MeLLo-style reasoning. Retrieval/KG-based (no weight edit).
- [UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET] MQuAKE-CF, k=3000 edited instances at once: GMeLLo 49.0% with GPT-J-6B (vs MeLLo 9.8%), 41.9% with Vicuna-7B (vs MeLLo 10.2%); k=1→3000 GMeLLo 76.3%→49.0%, MeLLo 20.3%→9.8% (GPT-J). NOTE these MeLLo numbers differ from MQuAKE v3 Table 5 (GPT-J 38.9→14.2), i.e., the dataset version (CF-3k vs CF-3k-v2) / prompt / metric differ across papers — cross-paper comparison unreliable.

### NOVELTY-CHECK PRIOR ART (collected)
- Continual Learning via Sparse Memory Finetuning (FAIR Meta + UC Berkeley; arXiv 2510.15103, Oct 2025) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.15103 : uses memory-layer (product-key style, Berges et al. 2024 "Memory Layers at Scale") models; updates only memory slots highly activated by new knowledge relative to pretraining usage (TF-IDF-like ranking). NaturalQuestions F1 drop after learning new facts: full FT −89%, LoRA −71%, sparse memory FT −11% at same new-knowledge acquisition. Values updated by gradient (SGD), not by exact LS; memory is part of a pretrained memory-layer model (not zero-init add-on). Follow-ups (not read): "Improving Sparse Memory Finetuning" (arXiv 2604.05248), "Sparse Memory Finetuning as a Low-Forgetting Alternative to LoRA and Full Finetuning" (arXiv 2605.03229).
- Larimar (ICML 2024; IBM; arXiv 2403.11901) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2403.11901 : distributed episodic memory (Kanerva-machine-style) attached to a frozen/decoder LLM; one-shot memory writes (least-squares/pseudo-inverse based write in the Kanerva-machine formulation — mechanism recalled, not re-verified here), 8–10x speed-ups; explicit "selective fact forgetting" and leakage prevention. → prior art for closed-form memory writes + deletion in an add-on memory.
- KBLaM (ICLR 2025; Microsoft; arXiv 2410.10450) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2410.10450 : each KB triple → continuous key-value vectors via pretrained sentence encoder + linear adapters; key from (name, property), value from the object; injected via rectangular attention; >10K triples into an 8B LLM on one A100; dynamic updates without fine-tuning. → prior art for "keys built from subject name + relation with a separate encoder".
- UltraEdit (2505.14679): closed-form ridge LS per turn, Δ = (HᵀH + I)⁻¹HᵀV, with lifelong running normalization (whitening) — ridge solve but only over the current batch, dense in-place weights.
- RLSEdit (2601.15686, Jan 2026): exact cumulative ridge LS over all edits via recursive least squares (Woodbury) on a dense MLP matrix — closest prior art for "joint LS over every fact stored so far".
- MEMIT (ICLR 2023) / AlphaEdit Eq.15: batch LS over current edits K1 with covariance of preserved keys K0 and previously edited keys Kp — joint LS within a batch; sequential application is not a full re-solve.
- GRACE (NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2211.11031): discrete key-value codebook at one layer; keys = cached activations, deferral radius ε; values trained per edit; memorizes but generalizes poorly (WISE Table 2: LLaMA-2-7B T=1000 Rel .93 / Gen .08 / Loc 1.00).
- WISE (NeurIPS 2024): side-memory FFN copy + router + random-mask sharding + Ties-merge.
- MEMOIR (NeurIPS 2025): residual memory with sample-dependent sparse masks (TopHash) and activation-pattern retrieval.
- MELO (AAAI 2024; arXiv 2312.11795): neuron-indexed dynamic LoRA blocks retrieved via a vector database of keys.
- T-Patcher (ICLR 2023; arXiv 2301.09785): adds one trainable neuron ("patch") per mistake in the last FFN layer — per-edit key/value neuron; no joint re-solve.
- MeG (ICLR 2026): single dynamic neuron whose weights come from a diffusion model conditioned on the query; familiarity network gates.
- [UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET, very close] "Ordo-M: An Externally Addressed Sparse Memory Grafted onto a Frozen Language Model" (Aug 2026, found only on alphaXiv with a non-standard id: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.ordo-m-sparse-memory-frozen-model): table of trainable value slots inserted into the forward pass of a frozen LLM; addresses are symbolic/lexical, derived from entity names in the prompt (entity name = unique key to a slot); deterministic addressing → "zero drift"/"zero-collateral edits" (previously stored records unchanged across sequential updates); 32 measurement rounds on three real domains + two standard editing benchmarks; CounterFact recall 89.0% at the entity's own address vs 2.2% at a foreign address; aimed at single consumer GPU users. Could not verify whether values are solved by LS or gradient, whether relation-binding or deletion is included.
- [UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET] Zenodo record 20258685 (May 2026) "Trainable Sparse Vector Memory for Frozen Language Models: A Controlled Synthetic Binding Study with Qwen2.5-0.5B": frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B + router MLP, trainable keys, trainable sparse value table, value projection, sigmoid gate; retrieved values injected as a residual update after transformer layer 8; synthetic entity-code bindings (ENTITY-000042 → FROST-32); 100-fact task 1.00 EM seen / 0.00 unseen. — https://zenodo.org/records/20258685
- Other 2026 "memory grafted onto frozen LM" work (titles only, not read): "Trained Persistent Memory for Frozen Decoder-Only LLMs" (arXiv 2603.22329), "... Frozen Encoder–Decoder LLMs: Six Architectural Methods" (arXiv 2603.16413), "Memory Grafting: Scaling LM Pre-training via Offline Conditional Memory" (arXiv 2605.20948), "Knowledge Offloading: Decomposing LLMs into Sparse Backbones and Memory Modules" (arXiv 2605.29075); US patent 12450168 "Dynamic updating of content addressable associative memories for large language models" (assignee not checked).
- Deletion/certification caveat: "Suppressed, Not Erased: A Representational Trace of Edited Facts Survives Even Weight-Free Knowledge Editing" (arXiv 2609.18985, Sep 2026; not read) — per search snippet argues that if an edit leaves the original object linearly decodable the "edit certificate is incomplete" → relevant to claims of "exact deletion" (deleting a memory entry ≠ the base model forgetting the original fact).
