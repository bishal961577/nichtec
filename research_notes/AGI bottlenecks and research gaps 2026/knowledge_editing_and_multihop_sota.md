### NAS — Norm-Anchor Scaling (arXiv 2602.02543)
- Title on arXiv/HF: "Toward Ultra-Long-Horizon Sequential Model Editing" (Liu, Zhu, Miao, Fujisawa; ICML-style template). The user-supplied title "Norm Anchors Make Model Edits Last" does NOT match the version indexed on HF (could be a revised title/venue version; unverified). — https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.02543
- Claim: L&E update rules induce exponential growth of edited MLP weight norm under sequential edits; NAS rescales each target value vector v_new to an anchor norm a = E||v_new|| measured by N=1000 pilot edits on the clean model; "one line of code". Proven bounded weight norm. Default "NAS" = AlphaEdit + NAS.
- Setting: atomic sequential editing (1 edit per step, weights committed after every edit) on the FULL CounterFact stream (20,877 edits) and FULL ZsRE stream (19,086 edits in text; 19,082 in Fig.1 caption — internal inconsistency). Models: Llama3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, GPT-J (GPT2-XL in appendix). Single H100.
- Table 1 (end of full stream), CounterFact Eff/Gen/Spe/Flu/Consis; ZsRE Eff/Gen/Spe:
  - Llama3-8B: NAS 97.95/82.71/60.44/619.72/31.88; ZsRE 93.17/88.30/32.15. AlphaEdit 66.27/58.75/49.98/555.21/4.12; ZsRE 22.30/20.34/1.57. RLEdit CF 65.38/49.18/47.86; ZsRE 81.59/78.77/27.03. UltraEdit CF 60.06/52.90/43.88; ZsRE 77.61/75.15/50.26. MEMIT CF 50.95/50.95/49.05; ZsRE 0.95/0.22/0.22. Pre-edited CF Spe 89.79, ZsRE Spe 32.09.
  - Qwen2.5-7B: NAS CF 90.44/55.05/73.21; ZsRE 96.84/88.10/42.52. AlphaEdit CF 84.59/58.41/68.96; ZsRE 97.25/86.17/40.16 (AlphaEdit survives on Qwen). RLEdit ZsRE 93.75/85.37/35.61.
  - GPT-J: NAS CF 98.73/90.68/60.75; ZsRE 92.08/85.04/22.24. AlphaEdit CF 62.65/54.57/54.73; ZsRE 86.13/79.43/21.84. RLEdit CF 78.10/54.86/48.78.
- Note: CounterFact specificity for NAS (~60) is far below the pre-edit model (~84–90) after 20k edits → locality is substantially damaged even for the best method.
- Collapse point (CP@60, CounterFact score ≤60): MEMIT & PRUNE 400→1.5k with NAS; RECT 400→1.4k; AlphaEdit 4k→>21k. GLUE-style capability: prior L&E baselines drop to near zero within ≤4k edits; NAS keeps non-trivial GLUE through 20,877 edits (MRPC noticeably declines).
- Baselines GRACE, MELO, WISE, MEMOIR, ENCORE, LyapLock only in Appendix C.4 (not read).
- Stated limitation: fixed anchor may be suboptimal for non-stationary streams / heterogeneous edit difficulty.
- CounterFact metrics here are teacher-forced probability comparisons (P[new] > P[old]); ZsRE is top-1 token accuracy (teacher-forced) — see the "mirage" critique.

### UltraEdit (arXiv 2505.14679; Gu, Huang, Gu, Zhang; reviewed on OpenReview id GoJLp3BIRV — venue listed as TMLR-style "Reviewed on OpenReview") — https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679
- Method: training-free, "subject-free", memory-free; per turn, hidden state h_i (at answer-label position) + gradient ∇y_i are concatenated, "lifelong normalization" (running mean/var across all turns), then a closed-form RIDGE least-squares update per module: Δθ = (HᵀH + I)⁻¹ HᵀV (Eq. 6–7) solved over the CURRENT TURN's batch only (not over all past edits). Authors frame it as MEMIT-like with whitened covariance.
- Scale: UltraEditBench (Wikidata5M triples, questions generated by GPT-4o-mini) with >2M editing pairs; UltraEdit* evaluated at 100K edits (ZsRE, FEVER), 500K (WikiBigEdit), 2M (UltraEditBench). Baselines only at 20K (ZsRE/FEVER/UltraEditBench) and 17K (WikiBigEdit). 100 edits per turn (WISE: 1 per turn). Metric: Exact Match (token-level, following AlphaEdit/RLEdit), plus WILD LLM-as-judge complementary.
- Table 2 (20K ZsRE edits unless *), Eff/Gen/Spe:
  - GPT-J: UltraEdit ZsRE 78.03/72.42/27.05; UltraEdit* (100K) 72.95/68.68/25.91; RLEdit 73.34/68.93/22.00; AlphaEdit 50.23/43.31/12.54; WISE 34.13/33.14/26.81.
  - Mistral-7B-v0.3: UltraEdit 85.30/80.80/47.38; AlphaEdit 0.00/0.00/0.00 (collapsed); RLEdit 72.57/68.87/23.17.
  - LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct: UltraEdit 90.07/87.36/49.51; UltraEdit* (100K) 87.80/85.48/46.74; RLEdit 91.34/89.68/41.94; AlphaEdit 74.34/67.85/22.94; WISE 40.94/40.27/37.40.
  - WikiBigEdit (17K, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct): UltraEdit Eff/Gen/Spe/Personas/Reasoning 79.60/73.49/48.51/66.55/35.64; UltraEdit* (500K) 68.99/63.59/52.28/55.04/–; RLEdit 75.35/70.00/37.21/65.55/28.13; AlphaEdit 63.24/54.68/20.17/42.58/0.01.
- Table 3 UltraEditBench, UltraEdit* at 2M edits, Eff/Gen/Spe: GPT-J 81.65/76.80/76.44; Mistral-7B 81.70/77.25/77.09; LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct 83.45/79.11/78.05; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 80.70/75.78/76.01. (At 20K: RLEdit LLaMA-3 85.69/81.88/65.64; AlphaEdit LLaMA-3 4.51/3.16/2.78; WISE LLaMA-3 42.27/41.65/39.79.)
- Claims 7x faster than prior SOTA and 4x less VRAM; "only method" to edit a 7B model on 24GB GPU.
- Caveat (inferred): WikiBigEdit multi-hop "Reasoning" stays ~30–36% — sequential edits do not propagate to multi-hop questions. Note that NAS paper (2602.02543) re-ran UltraEdit with 1 edit/step on full CounterFact and found much weaker numbers (Llama3 CF 60.06/52.90/43.88) — results depend heavily on batch-per-turn protocol and metric.

### MEMOIR (NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2506.07899; Wang K., Qin Y., Dimitriadis, Favero, Frossard — EPFL) 
- Primary PDF/OpenReview/project page blocked by proxy in this environment; HF papers has no copy. Numbers below are from web-search snippets of the arXiv abstract/NeurIPS page (NOT verified against paper tables):
  - Residual memory = dedicated parameter module added (a copy-style residual FFN projection); edits confined to sparse, sample-dependent masks of input activations ("TopHash"); at inference, compares sparse activation pattern of query to stored patterns to decide which memory to activate (threshold `irr_threshold` in repo). — https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/115624 ; https://github.com/qym7/MEMOIR
  - Reported (snippet): ZsRE 1,000 sequential edits on LLaMA-3(-8B): average score 0.93, +14 points over next-best AlphaEdit; at 15,000 edits MEMOIR average 0.88 while MEMIT/AlphaEdit collapse to near zero after a few thousand edits. Evaluated on LLaMA-3 and Mistral; QA (ZsRE), hallucination correction (SelfCheckGPT), OOD (Temporal). [UNVERIFIED against primary tables]
  - Repo README demos 100 sequential edits on ZsRE and SelfCheckGPT with Llama-3-8B; built on EasyEdit. — https://github.com/qym7/MEMOIR

### RLSEdit (arXiv 2601.15686, Jan 2026; Wang, Lyu, Gu, Huang, Lu, Cui, Chang — McGill/Mila) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.15686
- VERY RELEVANT TO NOVELTY CHECK: formulates lifelong editing as ONLINE REGULARIZED LEAST SQUARES over a CUMULATIVE key-value fitting objective (all edits so far) with two regularizers (deviation from pre-trained weights; deviation from an anchor mapping), solved by recursive least squares via Woodbury identity; per-edit cost independent of history length. Provides deviation bounds, asymptotic adherence–preservation characterization. "Soft constraints" vs AlphaEdit "hard preservation" and L&E "hard writes".
- Claims stable scaling to 10K edits on Llama-3 and Qwen2.5; retains early edits; preserves GLUE and held-out reasoning/code benchmarks. (Table numbers not yet read.)
- Difference vs user's method: RLSEdit edits a dense existing MLP weight matrix (in-place), recursive (order-dependent only through regularizer/anchor? exact RLS is order-independent for pure LS), no sparse slot memory, no explicit deletion or capacity certificate (not claimed in abstract).

### EtCon (arXiv 2512.04753, Dec 2025; Li R. et al., Wuhan U./Shanghai Innovation Inst./SJTU) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.04753
- Edit-then-Consolidate: (1) Targeted Proximal SFT (TPSFT) with trust-region objective to limit policy drift; (2) consolidation with GRPO aligning edited knowledge with CoT inference under autoregressive generation. Motivation: gap between teacher-forcing evaluation and real-world lifelong autoregressive effectiveness; overfitting; lack of consolidation stage. (Numbers not yet read.)

### WikiBigEdit (ICML 2025; arXiv 2503.05683; Thede, Roth, Bethge, Akata, Hartvigsen) — https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.05683
- >500K QA pairs (~7M tokens) from real Wikidata diffs over 8 time intervals, Feb–Jul 2024; auto-extending pipeline; evaluation axes: edit, generalization (rephrase), personas, locality, multi-hop reasoning. Notes other benchmarks small: CounterFact 20K, ZsRE 1K (as commonly used), SelfCheckGPT 600.

### WikiBigEdit key finding (primary text)
- "Our study reveals limited transfer of knowledge editing techniques to practically scalable lifelong updates and highlights that even when evaluated around knowledge editing desiderata, standard approaches such as retrieval augmentation or continual finetuning with model merging can perform better." — https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.05683 (ICML 2025). Per-method numbers not extracted (gap).

### MeG — Massive Editing via Dynamic Weight Generation (ICLR 2026; arXiv 2512.14395; code github.com/RodeWayne/MeG-for-Knowledge-Editing)
- Attaches ONE dynamic-weight neuron to specific layer(s); a diffusion model conditionally generates that neuron's weights from the input query; a "familiarity network" decides whether the query concerns edited knowledge. Pipeline trains: text encoder → familiarity network → neuron weights → weight-generation (diffusion) model — i.e., trained on the full edit set (batch/"massive" editing, compared against MALMEN), NOT an online sequential editor (inferred from README pipeline; confirm). — https://github.com/RodeWayne/MeG-for-Knowledge-Editing
- Reported (search snippet of arXiv v4, not primary-table verified): ZsRE & CounterFact, 1,024→10,000 edits; ZsRE 10,000 edits Score 82.80 on Phi-2 vs MALMEN 45.64; Locality on GPT-J 83.99%. — https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14395v4 [UNVERIFIED: proxy blocks arXiv/OpenReview/ICLR proceedings]
