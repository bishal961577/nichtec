## Q1. What do key figures say are the main missing pieces? (dated, most recent first per person)

### Takeaway
The most-repeated missing piece is **continual learning / durable memory (learning after deployment)**. Karpathy, Dwarkesh Patel, Sutskever, Sutton, Silver, Hassabis, Legg, LeCun, the Hendrycks et al. group, DeepMind's 2026 framework and (reportedly) Altman all name it. The second is **generalization and sample-efficient adaptation to novelty** (Sutskever, Chollet, Sutton, LeCun, Marcus). The third is **reliability and consistency ("jagged intelligence") over long-horizon agentic work** (Hassabis, Karpathy, Marcus, Hendrycks). Lab leaders (Amodei, Legg, Brockman/Altman) mostly treat these gaps as engineering problems that will fall soon. Sutton, LeCun, Marcus, Sutskever and the AAAI survey majority say new ideas are required. The picture moved in September 2026: GPT-6 Astra's ARC-AGI-3 result led Chollet to pull his AGI forecast forward, and Brockman to say "Welcome to the AGI era".

**Richard Sutton and David Silver**
- [O] Apr 2025, "Welcome to the Era of Experience" (preprint of a chapter for the MIT Press book *Designing an Intelligence*; read in full): "while imitating humans is enough to reproduce many human capabilities to a competent level, this approach in isolation has not and likely cannot achieve superhuman intelligence... In key domains such as mathematics, coding, and science, the knowledge extracted from human data is rapidly approaching a limit... The pace of progress driven solely by supervised learning from human data is demonstrably slowing." They say agents must "inhabit streams of experience, rather than short snippets of interaction", with rewards "grounded in their experience of the environment, rather than coming from human prejudgement". Human-prejudged rewards create "an impenetrable ceiling on the agent's performance". Current RL is "designed for short episodes of ungrounded, human interaction... not suitable for long streams of grounded, autonomous interaction." — [DeepMind PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- Continual learning has become the consensus *named* bottleneck: optimists (Amodei, Legg, Hassabis) and skeptics (Sutton, LeCun, Karpathy, Dwarkesh) name the same gap. They disagree on how hard it is ("not as difficult as it seems" / "not that hard" vs "need a breakthrough" / "new architecture") and on whether it gates economic impact (Amodei: no; Dwarkesh: yes).
- The debate moved during 2026. Early 2026 was dominated by "jagged" diagnoses (Hassabis) and ARC-AGI-3's <1% scores. By Sep 2026, OpenAI leadership was claiming the "AGI era", and even Chollet and Marcus acknowledged a genuine step on interactive novel-environment reasoning, while still rejecting the AGI label. On positions, the camps are roughly lab leaders vs independent scientists, though Hassabis sits in between (scaling + 1–2 breakthroughs).
- On recency: Sutskever, Sutton and Karpathy statements are from late 2025, and I found no substantive 2026 update from any of them (see Gaps). Their diagnoses predate the 2026 ARC-AGI-3 jump.

## Q2. What does benchmark evidence show as of 2026?

### Takeaway
By Sep 2026, measurements show very rapid progress on bounded, verifiable tasks. ARC-AGI-2 is effectively saturated (~93% verified). ARC-AGI-3 went from <1% (Mar 2026) to 30.2% (Jul) to 62.7% (Sep, GPT-6 Astra, standard harness). METR's 50% time horizon exceeded what its task suite can measure (≥16 h). Measurements of **real-world end-to-end work and reliability** still show large gaps. The best Remote Labor Index automation rate is 15.8% (Jul 2026). 80%-reliability horizons are several times shorter than 50% horizons. Independently run SWE-bench Pro sits at ~51–61%. Calibration/hallucination remains weak, though improving. The first dedicated continual-learning benchmark (Jun 2026) found frontier systems capture only ~25% of available learning gains.

### Cited Findings

**Continual learning (direct measurement)**
- [M] Continual Learning Bench (CL-Bench; UC Berkeley/Snorkel/UW-Madison incl. Zaharia and Gonzalez; 5 Jun 2026). Six expert-validated domains with hidden latent structure; "gain" = stateful minus stateless reward, normalised by headroom. Models: Claude Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Gemini 3 Flash, GPT-5.4, with ICL, notepad, Mem0, ACE, Claude Code and Codex. **Best system (full-context ICL + Sonnet 4.6): normalized reward 22.3%, normalized gain 25.4%.** Dedicated memory systems did worse: ACE gain 8.6% at the highest cost ($62.8/run). Agents "frequently overfit to immediate observations or fail to reuse knowledge"; "frontier systems are rigid in early, incorrect beliefs, and struggle to update despite feedback". Conclusion: "reliable online adaptation remains an open problem." — [arXiv 2606.05661 (HF mirror)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661)
- [M] "A Definition of AGI" (Oct 2025): Long-Term Memory Storage scored 0/10 for both GPT-4 and GPT-5 (see Q4) — [arXiv 2510.18212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212)

- [M] AA-Omniscience (Artificial Analysis, 18 Nov 2025): 6,000 questions, 42 topics. **Composite indices disagree**
- [M] On GPT-6 Astra (Sep 2026): Epoch AI's composite of 50+ benchmarks puts Astra first (169 points, 267 models). Artificial Analysis rates Astra 61, level with its predecessor and behind Claude Fable 5.1 (66). — [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/benchmarks-disagree-on-gpt-6-astra-but-its-human-beating-efficiency-on-arc-agi-3-pulls-chollets-agi-forecast-forward/) [snippet]

### Inferences
- **The gap is no longer "can it solve hard closed problems"** (ARC-AGI-2 ~93%, HLE ~50–65%, SWE-bench Verified saturated). It is now **(a) reliability at long horizons**, **(b) acceptable end-to-end delivery of real work**, and **(c) learning from experience**:
  - (a) Many real tasks need 80–99% success, and 80% horizons are ~3–5x shorter than 50% horizons.
  - (b) RLI: ~84% of real projects still fail.
  - (c) CL-Bench captures ~25% of learnable gain; memory storage scores 0 on the CHC battery.
- ARC-AGI-3 was the main *measured* evidence for a novelty/adaptation gap in early 2026, and it narrowed dramatically by Sep 2026. Two things temper that result:
  - Large harness effects: 62.7% vs ~100% for the same model, depending on whether reasoning state is preserved.
  - Cost: ~$19–26k per full evaluation, which conflicts with ARC's own efficiency framing of intelligence.

  The fact that preserving state lifts the score to ~100% suggests within-episode memory/state management is a key lever, consistent with the continual-learning diagnosis.
- Several benchmarks are saturating or contaminated (SWE-bench Verified; ARC-AGI-1/2 per ARC Prize; METR suite above 16 h). As a result, *measurement itself* has become a bottleneck for judging AGI claims in late 2026. DeepMind (Mar 2026) says existing benchmarks are insufficient.

### Gaps
- No METR time-horizon measurement found for Claude Opus 5/5.5, Claude Fable 5/5.1 or GPT-6 Astra. METR's suite is saturated above ~16 h, and no replacement suite ("Time Horizon 2.0") was confirmed as released.
- Official HLE (CAIS/Scale) top score and calibration error for Sep 2026 models are not verified; only aggregator figures are available.
- ARC-AGI-3 official leaderboard values could not be read directly (arcprize.org blocked). The 62.7% vs 66% discrepancy and the exact definition of "96% of levels" are unresolved.
- Not researched: OpenAI GDPval results, OSWorld/computer-use success rates, Terminal-Bench 2.0, or lab system-card hallucination numbers for 2026 models. The International AI Safety Report 2026 (24 Feb 2026, [arXiv 2602.21012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012)) was only available as metadata.

## Q3. Which bottlenecks recur most, and where do experts disagree?

### Takeaway
Ranked by how often and how strongly they are cited (2024–Sep 2026):
1. **Continual learning / long-term memory** (near-universal; also measured as ~0 or weak).
2. **Generalization and sample-efficient adaptation to novelty.**
3. **Long-horizon reliability, consistency and agency.**
4. **World models / physical and visual grounding.**
5. **Hallucination, calibration and metacognition.**
6. **Learning paradigm beyond human data (grounded RL, value functions).**
7. **Compute, data and energy** (rarely named as the blocker by this expert set).

The core disagreement is whether these fall to **scaling plus engineering** (Amodei, Legg, OpenAI leadership, partly Hassabis) or need **new ideas or architectures** (Sutton, LeCun, Sutskever, Marcus, Karpathy, and 76% of AAAI survey respondents).

## Q4. Published AGI definitions / level frameworks and what they say is weakest

### Takeaway
Three main frameworks exist:
- **DeepMind's "Levels of AGI"** (2023/ICML 2024): a performance × generality matrix. It put 2023 frontier LLMs at Level 1 "Emerging AGI".
- **Hendrycks et al.'s "A Definition of AGI"** (Oct 2025): a CHC-based score. **GPT-4 27%, GPT-5 57%**, with **Long-Term Memory Storage 0%**, hallucination 0/4, visual reasoning 0/2 and Speed 3/10.
- **DeepMind's "Measuring Progress Toward AGI: A Cognitive Framework"** (Mar 2026): 10 faculties. It flags learning, metacognition, attention, executive function and social cognition as poorly measured, and notes current systems learn "only during training or in-context".

ARC Prize frames intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency. All frameworks agree the weakest link is the ability to *learn and retain new knowledge over time*.

### Cited Findings

AGI = "matching the cognitive versatility and proficiency of a well-educated adult". Ten CHC domains, each weighted 10%. — [arXiv 2510.18212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212); [agidefinition.ai](https://www.agidefinition.ai/)

    | Model | K | RW | M | R | WM | MS | MR | V | A | S | **Total** |

    | GPT-5 (2025, "Auto") | 9 | 10 | 10 | 7 | 4 | **0** | 4 | 4 | 6 | 3 | **57%** |

- [O] **ARC Prize / Chollet framing**: intelligence = skill-acquisition efficiency on novel tasks using only core-knowledge priors. ARC-AGI-3 subsumes data, time, compute and risk into a single "action efficiency" measure and "penalizes brute-forcing". Stated position: "future AGI systems will not need task-specific external handholding to approach new tasks." — [arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621); [ARC-AGI-2 paper, arXiv 2505.11831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11831)
- [O] **Other operational definitions**, as catalogued in Levels of AGI:
  - OpenAI charter: "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work".
  - Legg/Goertzel: machines that can do the cognitive tasks people typically do.
  - Shanahan: can "learn to perform as broad a range of tasks as a human" (includes learning as a requirement).
  - Marcus: "flexible and general, with resourcefulness and reliability comparable to (or beyond) human intelligence", plus five tests (understand a movie, understand a novel, cook in an arbitrary kitchen, write a bug-free 10,000-line program, formalize proofs).
  - Suleyman: "Artificial Capable Intelligence" / Modern Turing Test (turn $100k into $1M).

  — [arXiv 2311.02462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02462)

### Inferences
- All three measurement-oriented frameworks (CHC-based, DeepMind cognitive, ARC) converge on *learning new things after training and retaining them* as the weakest or least-covered faculty. That is the same bottleneck experts rank #1 in Q3, so opinions and measurements agree here.
- The frameworks also show that AGI claims depend heavily on the definition. An economic-work definition (OpenAI charter) can be argued to be approaching, given Brockman's Sep 2026 claim. A "match a median/well-educated adult on *all* faculties" definition (Hendrycks, DeepMind 2026) cannot be passed while Long-Term Memory Storage is ~0. The RLI (15.8%) measures economic work directly and still shows most real projects failing.
- Hendrycks-style scores were only published for GPT-4 and GPT-5 (Oct 2025). Newer models (Opus 5.x, Fable 5.x, GPT-6 Astra) could have moved several domains (visual, reasoning, speed), but no authoritative re-scoring exists. Any claim about their "AGI %" is unverified.
