Labels: **[checked]** means I verified it, this session or in round 1. **[recall]** means it comes from memory.

### 1. Where I agree and disagree

All four of us, and the moderator, agree on the core point: the 4B's 50.2% measures a fast, thinking-off "DONE" gate over an index of one-fact notes, not search. Moderator check 4 sharpens it. Plain BM25 over person pages finds the right page for 97.5–100% of described-person questions. So in this world the index failed, and road B is close to the perfect-pages oracle. **The clean world cannot test B at all, so the next test must be messy.**

**What the others got wrong:**
- **Founder: "reading is solved at phone size."** It is solved for about 180 tokens of template text about two people. It is not solved for 60K tokens, messy chat or 4-bit weights.
- **Architect: HippoRAG 2 "about 7 points on multi-hop".** The paper's F1 table [checked] gives +2.8 on average over NV-Embed-v2, and +4.2 on the three multi-hop sets. The "7%" is the abstract's headline, which I believe is a retrieval-recall measure [recall].
- **Architect: "graph memory's edge came from weak 2024 long-context models".** Only partly. In MemoryAgentBench (2025), HippoRAG-v2 scores 65.1 against GPT-4o's long-context 58.1 on accurate retrieval, but Mem0 scores 32.6 and Zep 37.5 [checked]. The edge belongs to particular retriever designs, not to structure as such.
- **Neuroscientist: Haiku's missing thinking text.** That is by design; amendment 2 says Claude's reasoning text is not returned. It is not the vLLM bug.
- **Neuroscientist: AMA-Bench.** Its abstract does not say that the whole history beats structured memory [checked]. It reports its own causal-graph agent at 57.22%, 11.16 points above the strongest baseline.
- **Neuroscientist: "index, not rewritten profiles".** I strongly agree. It matches LongMemEval's lesson: expand the keys with facts, but keep the raw text as the values [recall].
- **Everyone, including me:** four experts voting D is a consensus built on a shared prior, not evidence.

### 3. Line for the pending 4B whole-notebook arm (commit before it runs)

Hard set, automatic score; perfect pages are 99.9%.
- **PASS (A viable as the phone's working-set reader at 60K):** all of these must hold:
  - at least 85%;
  - described people, who-is and two-hop each at least 75%;
  - at most 2% of answers cut off at the 16K cap;
  - median thinking at most 1,500 tokens.
- **FAIL:** at most 70%. A on the phone is limited to a small recent window, and selection is the critical path.
- **Between, or ≥ 85% but median thinking over 1,500 tokens:** "reads, but too slow as-is". A becomes a window, sized by the 100-note arm.
- **Also report:**
  - the matched 635 name-keyed questions, against Haiku's 93.4%;
  - the 90th percentile of thinking length.
- **Note in the line:** a pass is at 16-bit precision only. 4-bit weights lost up to 59% on long inputs, and up to 23% on average at 128K, in Mekala et al., EMNLP 2025 [checked, arXiv 2505.20276].

### 5. The merged test

**Keep:**
- **Notebook (architect's, generated by code so the truth is exact):** pronouns, nicknames, "my sister", the same name within one circle, plain-restatement updates, deletions.
- **Fan effect (neuroscientist, founder):** 10 people with 300+ notes each.
- **Paraphrased slice:** 1,000 notes rewritten by the 27B on the L40S, each checked automatically to keep its value, and reported separately.
- **Write side (neuroscientist):** an index of rows (person, slot, value, note id, position), never overwritten, built by the 4B.
- **Read side:** intersection of value cues, one link hop, then the raw notes are read.
- **Measures:** deletion audit and confident-wrong rate (founder); write tokens per note and wrong merges (architect).

**Arms (Qwen3.5-4B):**
- B-index;
- an oracle index built by code (separates writing errors from reading errors);
- C: an agent with thinking on that searches the raw notes with BM25 plus embeddings, up to 4 rounds, with the same token budget as B;
- A: the whole notebook;
- perfect pages;
- B and C again with thinking off, 256-token cap.

**My guard against schema leakage:**
- slots are open-vocabulary;
- 2 attribute types and 2 relation types appear only in the test world;
- code and prompts are frozen before the test world is generated with a fresh seed.

**Drop:** the 15K-note scale arm (move it to the next test); HippoRAG 2 (run it only if B passes, as the novelty check); thinking-off accuracy and answer length as pass conditions (report them only).

**PASS (choose B):** all of these must hold:
- B at least 85% on the hard set, and at least 0.9 × the oracle index;
- B at least 5 points above C on described, who-is and two-hop questions taken together (paired bootstrap; the interval excludes 0);
- wrong merges at most 1% overall, and at most 3% on near-name pairs;
- 0 deleted facts come back.

**If B passes every condition except the 5-point margin:** choose C, which is simpler.

**STOP:** B below 70%, wrong merges above 3%, or any leak caused by the design. If C is also below 70%, the 4B cannot run its own memory. The options are then the 27B as a nightly writer, or A as a window.

**Cost:**
- about 8–12 L40S-hours, roughly $15–25 [recall on the price];
- 1–2 weeks of code;
- scoring by code, so no paid API is needed.

### 6. What would change my mind, and the risk nobody named

**What would change my mind:** C within 3 points of B on the association kinds of the messy notebook. The owner's linking would then add machinery and permanent merge risk for no gain, and I would vote C plus an A window. Letta's 74.0% with plain file search on LoCoMo makes this about 40% likely [vendor blog].

**The biggest unnamed risk is designer's advantage.** Every pass in this programme has been on a world the team wrote. B's slots will quietly mirror the generator's 9 attributes and 10 relations, and pass for that reason. The guard in section 5 exists to counter it.

**Second risk, which the architect touched but nobody has proposed testing:** 4-bit quantization at long context hits road A hardest. One 4-bit whole-notebook arm would cost under an hour.
