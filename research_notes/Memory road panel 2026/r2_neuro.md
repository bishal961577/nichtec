## 1. Where I agree and disagree with the other three

**Agreement.** All four of us found the same handicap in search2 independently. All three others also land on D, with the raw notes as the truth and pages as an index that can be rebuilt. I agree with that.

**Architect.** His one-page-per-person check is the most useful new fact (moderator check 4) [checked]:
- the right page ranked first for 97.5–100% of described, "who" and first-name questions;
- this is binding at work: storing the two facts as one unit turns a two-feature description into a single match.

Two corrections:
- It is easy here only because every note carries the full name.
- Each page is small (at most 44 notes). Once a close person has thousands of notes, her page is linked to everything, and the fan effect returns inside the page [recall]. "This test cannot judge B" is right about writing the pages, but not about the benefit of binding.

**Researcher.** He says 50.2% is "mostly BM25 recall". That understates the gate. When the 4B did search again [checked, moderator check 1]:
- two-hop went from 1% to 54%;
- first name went from 49% to 89%.

The gate and the index both cost points. His six-arm test (including running HippoRAG 2 locally and a 15K-note notebook) is too big for a team whose last run lost points to a token underestimate and two bugs.

**Founder.** "Reading is solved at phone size" is true only for choosing between two people. The 4B has never been measured on hard questions among similar distractors: the 100- and 1,000-note arms are name-keyed only [checked]. I agree that deletion is the measure that defines trust.

The mechanism matters for his thinking-off pass line:
- Fast, familiarity-style recall handles direct cues.
- Composition (two-hop) needs deliberate retrieval, step by step.
- So measure thinking off as a separate arm, not as the pass line.

**A new fact for everyone** [checked]. The 4B's median output on the hard set is:
- 709 tokens with perfect pages;
- 2,605 tokens with search2;
- 3,574 tokens with bm25.

When the answer is missing, it thinks 4–5 times longer. That is the slow, exhaustive "no" from memory-search experiments [recall], and on a phone it means minutes per answer.

My D has four parts:

## 3. Line for the pending 4B whole-notebook run (hard set, automatic score)

**Pass, X = 90%.** "A holds at 60K tokens on the 4B" only if all of these hold:
- described, "who" and two-hop each ≥ 80%;
- median completion ≤ 1,500 tokens, about 2× the 709 on perfect pages;
- ≤ 2% of answers hit the 16K cap.

**Stop, Y = 75%.** At or below this, A is out as the phone's reader at 5,000 notes.

**Accurate but slow.** If accuracy is ≥ 90% but the length condition fails, the verdict is "cloud or overnight only".

**Why the thinking-length ratio matters.** Similar length to perfect pages means attention retrieves in parallel, the way memory is addressed by content. Much longer means the model is searching serially in its thinking, and that cost grows with the notebook.

## 5. The merged next test

**Keep:**

It has first names, nicknames, pronouns, "my sister", same-name people inside one circle, 3–6 links per person, many notes for close people, plain-restatement updates, and 100 deletions. Gold labels exist for every mention.

- **Comparison arms:**
  - oracle index built by code, to separate writing errors from finding errors;

  - A, the whole notebook cached;

**Drop for now:** HippoRAG 2, the 15K notebook, fused search with a reranker, and paraphrasing by a model.

**Pass:** all of these must hold.

## 6. What would change my mind, and the unnamed risk

**What would change my mind:** wrong merges above 3% on same-name pairs that a nightly rebuild cannot fix. Then binding at write time is unsafe, and I would move to A plus search.

**The biggest risk nobody has named: the test world is purely semantic** (a 9-column table about people). Real questions to a companion are mostly episodic and cued by time and context: "the restaurant Mira mentioned when we were in Lisbon", "what I told you last week". The cues are also relative to the user and change with time ("my boss" in 2026 is not "my boss" in 2029).

Person pages turn episodes into gist and drop the context those cues need. Zep's drop from 94.6 to 80.4 on single-session questions is that loss [checked via the researcher]. The hippocampal index is about what, where and when, not about people. So the index must key on time and episode too, and the next notebook needs episodic questions.
