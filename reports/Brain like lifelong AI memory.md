# Write Fast by Day, Consolidate at Night

The best-supported way to get teach-once, search-once learning that lives in a phone's own weights is a **two-speed parametric memory around a frozen 2–4B base model**. By day, a hypernetwork turns each taught fact or fetched web page into a small additive adapter block in about one prefill; Doc-to-LoRA does this in 0.09–0.55 s on a GPU at 82–87% of in-context accuracy ([D2L](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.15902)). By night, while charging, the day's episodes are consolidated into a fixed-size sparse memory layer through self-generated, bidirectional study items, self-distillation, spaced and targeted replay, and a regression gate that rolls back bad nights, with an append-only journal of raw episodes (never read when answering) supplying replay, deletion and rebuilds. Each part is measured in isolation: sparse memory-slot writes cut held-out forgetting to an 11% F1 drop versus 89% for full fine-tuning and 71% for LoRA ([SMF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.15103)), diverse rewrites lift extractable recall from 9.7% to 96.6% ([Physics 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316)), and self-distillation reaches 89% on post-cutoff facts versus 80% for SFT ([SDFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897)). Nobody has run the combination, nobody has shown sparse-slot writes beyond about a thousand facts, and three problems remain unsolved in every line of work: multi-hop use of written facts (edited GPT-J falls from 40.5% to 7–8% on MQuAKE ([MQuAKE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795))), overriding strong prior beliefs (16% on the strongest-prior quartile, 71–72.5% after the best fix ([Override Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.23750))), and skills as opposed to facts. On a phone, capacity is not the limit — a 1M-slot int8 memory layer holds roughly 13 million facts at 1 bit per parameter after a memory-efficiency discount, centuries of heavy personal use — but nightly training throughput is: the ~4 tokens/s measured for on-phone fine-tuning sustains about a dozen new facts per night, while 50 tokens/s would sustain a heavy user's ~100 facts per day at about 0.3 Wh per fact and 24–40 Wh per night from the wall. Novelty can be claimed only for the integration and for measuring how repeated searches fall over time, and the first experiment is cheap and decisive: stream 100,000 facts through the sleep recipe into a sparse memory layer and check whether the earliest facts survive past the ~2,000-fact point where slot writes must begin to overlap.

## Nine research lines each solve one piece, and none solves the whole

The notes cover nine lines of work; each solves one sub-problem of the target behaviour on its own benchmarks, and none delivers teach-once, search-once, lifelong learning into the weights of a device. Evidence labels follow the notes: unmarked numbers were read in the primary paper's full text, **[abstract]** marks numbers from the primary abstract only, **[S]** marks secondary sources (a search snippet, a vendor or press page, or an official page seen only through a snippet) that were not checked against the full source, and **[calc]** marks arithmetic done in the notes or in this report from cited figures. Results from this project's own small-scale NumPy experiments ([AGI_BOTTLENECK.md](../AGI_BOTTLENECK.md), [COUNTING_AI.md](../COUNTING_AI.md)) appear only where they bear on a design choice and are labelled as this project's own.

### Knowledge editors write single facts fast but collapse or stay shallow at scale

Knowledge editing asks how to change one fact inside existing weights without retraining. ROME locates facts in mid-layer MLPs and writes a rank-one update, scoring **91.5 on CounterFact for GPT-J** (efficacy 99.9, paraphrase 99.1, neighbourhood specificity 78.9 against 83.0 unedited), while plain fine-tuning reaches 100% efficacy with only 10.3 specificity, breaking nearly 90% of neighbouring prompts ([ROME](https://huggingface.co/papers/2202.05262)). MEMIT spreads a batch across layers and keeps a **score of 85.8 after 10,000 batched edits** on GPT-J (specificity 73.7 against 83.5 unedited), but the batch took 7.44 hours on a GPU, about 2.7 s per edit [calc], and it stores only the subject→object direction ([MEMIT](https://huggingface.co/papers/2210.07229)).

Sequential use — the lifelong case — is where classic methods fail. ROME's efficacy holds near 100% until it breaks somewhere **between 100 and 1,000 edits**, and a single "disabling edit" can erase all earlier edits and downstream ability ([Gupta et al. 2024](https://huggingface.co/papers/2401.07453)); on real Wikidata updates ROME, R-ROME and MEMIT collapse "within the first few hundred updates" ([WikiBigEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.05683)). The 2025–26 fixes push the horizon out. Null-space projection (AlphaEdit) keeps LLaMA3-8B at 98.90 efficacy, 94.22 generalization and 67.88 specificity after 2,000 sequential edits where MEMIT falls to 65.65, 64.65 and 51.56 ([AlphaEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2410.02355)), though it declines by 8,000 edits [S] ([RLEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05759)) and scores 0.00 on Mistral-7B at 20,000 ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)). UltraEdit's closed-form update with "lifelong normalization" reports **83.45 / 79.11 / 78.05 on LLaMA-3-8B after 2 million edits** by exact match, and removing the normalization drops efficacy from 84.47 to 36.15 ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)); StableEdit also claims 2M sequential edits [S] ([StableEdit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11836)). NeuralDB replaces the linear update with a gated key-value module inside the FFN, loses only 3% from 2,000 to 10,000 facts, reaches **100,000 facts** and supports deletion ([NeuralDB](https://huggingface.co/papers/2507.18028)). The strongest result under realistic evaluation, however, is careful fine-tuning rather than an editor: LocFT-BF's breadth-first, mini-batch, localized tuning beats the next-best method by **33.72% average reliability at 3,000 edits** and reaches 100,000 sequential edits, with Qwen2.5-72B at 99.80% reliability but only **57.60% generalization** and capability 62.54 against 64.84 originally ([LocFT-BF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.22072)). LocFT-BF also reports that UltraEdit and RLEdit "struggle to scale" beyond 20,000 edits under autoregressive evaluation — a direct contradiction of UltraEdit's 2M headline that tracks the evaluation protocol, so the 2M figure is best read as an upper bound.

Adaptor-style editors leave base weights untouched and add a codebook or side memory with a router. GRACE keeps an edit retention rate of .96 after 1,000 edits on T5 ([GRACE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2211.11031)), but at 3,000 edits on LLaMA-2-7B its paraphrase generalization is **0.03**; WISE reaches 0.61 reliability, 0.58 generalization and 1.00 locality, and oracle routing would give 0.75 and 0.70, so the router is the bottleneck ([WISE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2405.14768)).

Headline editing numbers overstate usable knowledge. Replacing teacher forcing and target-length truncation with free generation and an LLM judge drops single-edit success **from 96.8% to 38.5%**, and after 1,000 sequential edits average realistic reliability is 0.065–0.124 across three 7–8B models ([Mirage](https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.11177)). Edited models still emit the target word 80–90% of the time under negation, and a method with 84% standard success scores 29.9% in a fact-check format [S] ([Built on Sand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00625)); about 70% of QA-format edits fail when queried as text completion ([Gupta et al. 2024](https://huggingface.co/papers/2401.07453)). Edited facts are also rarely used as premises. On MQuAKE, GPT-J's multi-hop accuracy falls **from 40.5% before editing to 7.0–8.0%** after ROME, MEMIT or MEND despite 88–96% edit-wise success, chain-of-thought lifts ROME only to 19.9%, and on real 2021→2023 changes ROME and MEMIT reach 0.3% multi-hop with 100% edit-wise success ([MQuAKE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795)). RippleEdits measures 38–66% on logical consequences, with a simple in-context baseline best ([RippleEdits](https://huggingface.co/papers/2307.12976)); the best weight-based propagation method, PropMEND, reaches 22.4% against 12.7% on non-verbatim questions and 17.7% on the hardest out-of-domain setting ([PropMEND](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.08920)); and at lifelong scale UltraEdit scores at most 35.80 on WikiBigEdit multi-hop while AlphaEdit scores about zero ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)). On WikiBigEdit, plain retrieval "nearly tripl[es]" editing accuracy and LoRA fine-tuning beats WISE and MEMIT at scale ([WikiBigEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.05683)). Editing is therefore ruled out as the lifelong write path; the lessons that carry over are LocFT-BF's (mini-batched, localized updates with replay of every stored fact) and the exact-deletion structure of NeuralDB and Larimar.

### Parametric memory modules trade permanence, capacity and write cost against each other

Memory modules add dedicated, writable capacity so new knowledge need not overwrite old, and they split into cheap forward-pass writers that decay or saturate and true parameters that need gradients. MemoryLLM attaches a latent pool of about a billion values (32 layers × 7,680 tokens × 4,096 dimensions) to Llama-2-7B and writes by forward pass with no backprop; it still functions after 650,000 writes, but forgetting is designed in — each write drops 256 of 7,680 tokens per layer at random, capping retention of a fact at 0.51 after 20 writes and 0.03 after 100 [calc] — and even the most recently injected fact is recalled only 46% of the time on NaturalQA and 39% on SQuAD ([MemoryLLM](https://huggingface.co/papers/2402.04624)). M+ extends retention past 160,000 tokens by moving dropped tokens to a CPU-side store of up to 150,000 tokens per layer with a co-trained retriever, effectively a latent vector database, and trails its base on the multi-hop LongBench sets ([M+](https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.00592)). Larimar writes facts gradient-free into a 512×768 matrix by closed-form least squares: 1.1 s for 10 edits on GPT-2 against 4.8 s for ROME and 13.9 s for GRACE, CounterFact 99.6 / 88.4 / 80.4 on the 6B model, edit retention 0.97 after 1,000 sequential edits, and **exact deletion** — recall of a deleted fact falls to 0.0–0.04 while retained facts stay at 0.997 — with capacity of about the slot count, near 100% up to 512 facts and 82% at 1,024 ([Larimar](https://huggingface.co/papers/2403.11901)). Memory³ and KBLaM are cheap writers but store knowledge as external key-value databases read by attention: Memory³ compresses 7.17 PB of key-values to 4.02 TB, adds 2.51 points on average and costs 35.2% of decode throughput ([Memory³](https://huggingface.co/papers/2407.01178)), and KBLaM holds more than 10,000 triples where in-context learning tops out near 200 ([KBLaM](https://huggingface.co/papers/2410.10450)).

True parametric capacity comes from product-key memory layers, which add millions of key-value slots at negligible FLOPs. A 1.3B model with a 1M-key Memory+ layer rises from **7.76 to 13.68 on NaturalQuestions**, and with 64M keys (138.7B total parameters) to 20.78, still below Llama2-7B's 25.10; memory models generally match dense models with twice the parameters ([Memory Layers at Scale](https://huggingface.co/papers/2412.09764)), and PEER's million single-neuron experts perform similarly ([PEER](https://huggingface.co/papers/2407.04153)). Sparse memory finetuning (SMF) turns such a layer into a post-deployment writer by updating, with plain SGD, only the top t=500 value rows that a new fact uses unusually often relative to pretraining usage (a TF-IDF score). On a 1.3B model with a 1M-slot layer learning 1,000 TriviaQA facts one per step with 64 paraphrases each, held-out NaturalQuestions F1 **drops 11%, against 89% for full fine-tuning and 71% for LoRA**, at the same acquisition, and the pattern holds for a stream of 1,824 document chunks ([SMF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.15103)). 2026 follow-ups retrofit memory layers into Qwen-2.5-0.5B and gain 2.5 points on MedMCQA with both forgetting probes within about 1 point [S] ([Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05248); [Gupta et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03229)). SMF's gaps are exactly the ones that matter here: it has been shown only up to about a thousand facts at 0.5–1.3B, it still costs 11% on held-out QA, it was never tested on multi-hop use, and its IDF background protects pretraining knowledge but not earlier user writes. On phone hardware, Samsung's MeKi shows that large per-layer lookup tables can live in ROM with "zero inference latency overhead": a 1.7B MeKi model averages 59.7 zero-shot against 60.5 for a 4B dense model while **decoding 2.26× faster on a Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU**, though its tables are fixed at training time ([MeKi](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.03359)). A 2026 study names the remaining hole the "Knowing–Using Gap": fine-tuned models memorise new facts quickly but fail to use them in 2-hop reasoning, and a heuristic recovers 58–75% of the oracle headroom [abstract] ([Knowing–Using Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08393)).

### Test-time learning compresses context into fast weights that are discarded afterwards

Test-time-learning architectures solve a different problem: compressing a long context into fast weights cheaply. Titans trains an MLP memory online with gradient steps, momentum ("surprise") and a weight-decay forget gate; in one Table 1 block (model size unlabelled in the parsed text) its MAC variant averages **52.51** against 49.69 for Gated DeltaNet and 47.32 for TTT, and it scales beyond 2M-token contexts ([Titans](https://huggingface.co/papers/2501.00663)). TTT-E2E, a 3B Transformer that keeps learning by next-token prediction on its context, matches full attention's loss scaling to 128K tokens at **2.7× the speed** by updating the MLPs in the last quarter of blocks, but full attention "dramatically outperforms" it on needle retrieval ([TTT-E2E](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.23675)). ARC test-time training reaches 53.0% with an 8B model and 61.9% ensembled [abstract] ([ARC TTT](https://huggingface.co/papers/2411.07279)). None of these keeps what it learns: the Nested Learning paper, by the Titans authors, calls test-time training and memorization "parametric in-context learning, where the acquired in-context knowledge does not persist once the current context is removed" ([Nested Learning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.24695)), TTT-E2E resets its updated MLPs at document boundaries, and HOPE's multi-timescale "continuum memory system", designed for persistence, has results that could not be verified here [S]. The design therefore does not use these as a permanent store; the observation that carries over is that TTT-E2E and In-Place TTT [abstract] write into MLP output projections, the same site Doc-to-LoRA writes into ([In-Place TTT](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.06169)).

### Hypernetwork writers turn a document into weights in under a second, at 82–87% of in-context accuracy

Amortized writers replace an optimization loop with one forward pass. MEND turned gradients into edits in 98 s per 10,000 edits, but scored 23.1 on a batch of 10,000 edits and forgets "almost instantaneously" under sequential edits ([MEMIT](https://huggingface.co/papers/2210.07229); [Gupta et al. 2024](https://huggingface.co/papers/2401.07453)); MALMEN edits "hundreds of times more facts than MEND" ([MALMEN](https://huggingface.co/papers/2311.04661)); and RLEdit, trained with RL over whole edit sequences, reaches LLaMA-3-8B ZsRE 91.34 / 89.68 / 41.94 at 20,000 edits ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)) but degrades beyond that under realistic evaluation ([LocFT-BF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.22072)). Their shared failure is drift, because "the hypernetwork remains fixed while the underlying model continues to evolve" ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)). Document writers avoid this by conditioning on a frozen model's activations. Generative Adapter maps context to adapter weights through a fixed-size additive accumulator, lifting StreamingQA closed-book F1 **from 19.5 (fine-tuning) to 31.5**, and on multi-session chat reaches F1 40.2 against 66.0 for full-conversation prompting at a quarter of the compute (0.505 vs 2.059 TFLOPs), working best under about 1K tokens ([Generative Adapter](https://huggingface.co/papers/2411.05877)). Text-to-LoRA produces skills, not knowledge — 67.7 zero-shot against 61.0 for 3-shot in-context learning — and "cannot effectively internalize knowledge" ([T2L](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.06105); [D2L](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.15902)).

Doc-to-LoRA (D2L) is the most relevant writer. A 309M-parameter Perceiver hypernetwork reads gemma-2-2b's own activations and emits a rank-8 LoRA on each layer's MLP down-projection. It reaches **82.5% of in-context accuracy on SQuAD in under a second** where oracle context distillation takes about 40 s; it scores 0.866 normalized in 0.086 s against 0.650 in 631 s for distillation with 100 generated queries; it scores **0.857 on 2WikiMultihopQA** documents up to 32K tokens with a 0.209 s update; its needle recall is near-perfect across **up to 40 concatenated chunk adapters (40K tokens)** despite training only on contexts of 256 tokens or fewer; and at a 128K haystack it needs under 50 MB instead of over 12 GB ([D2L](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.15902)). Train-time hypernetwork injection follows power-law scaling with better out-of-distribution exponents than LoRA or full fine-tuning [abstract] ([Scaling laws](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.19604)). The failures are specific. D2L-style adapters collapse to **46.4% on the deepest conflicts** with pretrained beliefs, falling from 68% on weak priors to 16% on the strongest, and training-free amplitude fixes raise deep-conflict accuracy only to 71.0% (Gemma-2B) and 72.5% (Mistral-7B) [abstract] ([Override Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.23750)); D2L "might assume that subsequent queries will always be related to the internalized knowledge"; and no one has published more than about 100 sequential hypernetwork writes into one model. Cartridges show the offline alternative — gradient-trained key-value prefixes that match in-context quality with 38.6× less memory and 26.4× higher throughput and compose by concatenation — at a high per-corpus training cost ([Cartridges](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.06266)).

### One exposure is not enough: facts need diverse, bidirectional rewrites

This line explains why a fact heard once does not become weights on its own. Allen-Zhu and Li show that without varied rewrites a fact is memorised but not extractable: QA accuracy on held-out people is **9.7% from single biographies and 96.6% with five diverse, sentence-shuffled versions**, with multiplicity alone giving 41% and shuffling alone 70% ([Physics 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316)). Capacity is about **2 bits per parameter at about 1,000 exposures per fact and about 1 bit at 100**, unchanged by int8 quantization but **cut to 0.7 bit/param by int4**; mixing useful and junk tokens 1:7 costs 20× capacity, reduced to 2× by prepending a source token ([Physics 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)). Inverse lookup is "virtually 0%" ([Physics 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14402)), and fine-tuning GPT-3-175B on "A is B" with 30 paraphrases per fact gives 96.7% in the same direction but near 0% for "B is ?", no better than random names, until the reverse form is itself trained ([Reversal curse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12288); [Reverse training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13799)).

The recipes that work generate diverse study material. EntiGraph expands a 1.3M-token corpus about 350× into 455M synthetic tokens and lifts Llama 3 8B closed-book QA from 39.49% to **56.22%**, over 80% of the gain RAG gives (60.35%) ([EntiGraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)). SEAL's RL-trained self-edits lift no-context SQuAD from 33.5% (training on the passage only) to **47.0%**, above GPT-4.1-written data (46.3%), while untrained self-generated implications give 39.7% ([SEAL](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.10943)). Active Reading raises SimpleWikiQA from 7.42% (base) and 15.92% (repeated text) to **63.33–66%** with 10% pretraining data mixed in ([Active Reading](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09494)). Self-distillation (SDFT), with the model conditioned on the source acting as its own teacher, reaches **89% on post-cutoff 2025 facts against 80% for SFT**, with near-perfect out-of-distribution accuracy and less forgetting ([SDFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897)), and training on QA before the documents adds 17.8% ([PIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12847)). Acquisition also decays: each exposure adds a small probability increment that is forgotten along a power law, faster for duplicated data ([Chang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11813)), and facts new to the model are learned more slowly and raise hallucination linearly as they are fitted [S] ([Gekhman et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.444/)). This line fails in cost and coverage: every working recipe spends many generated tokens per fact, no study measures the minimum number of variants needed for durable recall of a single fact, and on new current events RAG still leads fine-tuning 0.875 to 0.504 [S] ([Ovadia et al.](https://x.com/IntuitMachine/status/1750205032509681825)).

### Forgetting tracks how far the output distribution moves, and sparse slots move it least

The forgetting literature explains what makes a write destructive and what limits the damage. Forgetting tracks how far the model's output distribution moves on the new task — KL fits forgetting with **R² 0.96 on a toy task and 0.71 for LLMs** — and an SFT target chosen to minimise that KL forgets even less than RL ([RL's Razor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259)). LoRA "learns less and forgets less" than full fine-tuning, whose perturbations have 10–100× higher rank ([Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)). Replay is cheap insurance: even **1% replay significantly reduces forgetting** in LLM continual pretraining, 5% (weak shift) and 25% (strong shift) are the chosen defaults, 50% hurts adaptation, and production continual pretraining used 30% (DeepSeek-V2) and 60% (Zamba) ([Ibrahim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763)). Some forgetting is alignment rather than knowledge: safety drops from 100% to 0% after 10 identity-shifting examples and recovers to 99% after 10 safety examples, and freezing bottom layers raises sequential-finetuning accuracy from 11% to 44% ([Spurious forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13453)). Sparse slot writes give the largest measured reduction for fact streams (SMF's 11% against 71–89%, above).

Sequential streams without protection fail badly. SEAL retains only **1.30%** accuracy after 100 sequential passages, and SCoL's learned sparse layer selection with a retention reward raises that to 20.46%, still below the 28.17% of prompting with no update ([SCoL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07076)); sequential LoRA personalization of small models shows "hidden instability patterns" ([Stability monitoring](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27634)); parameter expansion is "necessary" to retain and learn at once ([CKL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03215)); and continual learners "fail to rectify" outdated facts because of small gradients [S] ([EvolvingQA](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.302/)). This project's own small-scale experiments sharpen one point: on identical sparse codes, an additive counting write rule scored the same in ordered and shuffled order (91.4% on MNIST) while an error-driven delta rule fell from 98.0% shuffled to **22.8% ordered**, and representation drift under stored memories was a second, separate source of forgetting ([AGI_BOTTLENECK.md](../AGI_BOTTLENECK.md)). Sparsity reduces interference; for error-driven writes such as SMF's SGD it does not remove it.

### Phones can hold the training job but run it hundreds of times slower than inference

2025–26 flagships have 12–16 GB of LPDDR5X at about 77–85 GB/s, 15.5–19.8 Wh batteries and a sustained thermal envelope of roughly 3–7 W [S] ([Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/iphone-17-on-device-inference-benchmarks); [GSMArena S26 Ultra](https://www.gsmarena.com/samsung_galaxy_s26_ultra_5g-14320.php); [wccftech](https://wccftech.com/new-results-show-smartphone-thermal-capabilities-have-hit-a-wall-in-cooling-chipsets/)), while mid-range phones such as the Pixel 10a and iPhone 17e have 8 GB [S] ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/03/05/iphone-17e-has-8gb-of-ram/)). Qualcomm measures 4-bit Llama models on the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 at **68.18 / 30.10 / 14.99 decode tokens/s for 1B / 3B / 8B** ([Qualcomm 3B card](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/Llama-v3.2-3B-Instruct)), and its time-to-first-token figures imply prefill of about 3,970 / 1,770 / 1,180 tokens/s [calc]. Measured decode energy is 0.257–0.672 J/token for a 7B-class model on a 2024 flagship [S] ([PowerInfer-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.06282)), and under sustained load GPU inference loses 40–60% of its throughput within about a minute while Apple's Neural Engine keeps 67% [S] ([sustained-load study](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23640v2); [ANE vs GPU](https://dev.to/john-rocky/iphone-on-device-llm-the-gpu-wins-the-sprint-the-neural-engine-wins-the-marathon-42lo)).

Training fits in memory: Apple's MeBP fine-tunes 0.5–4B models with LoRA in **under 1 GB on an iPhone 15 Pro Max** [S] ([MeBP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03425)), MeSP cuts a 0.5B job from 361 MB to 136 MB [S] ([MeSP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13069v1)), and a 3B model at 4K tokens fits in 1.59 GB with a 4-bit base and offloading [S] ([2606.19528](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19528v1)). Throughput is the problem. QVAC tuned a 1B BitNet model on about 18,000 tokens in 1 h 18 min on a Galaxy S25 and 1 h 45 min on an iPhone 16 [S] ([QVAC](https://huggingface.co/blog/qvac/fabric-llm-finetune-bitnet)), about **3.8 training tokens/s** [calc] and two to three orders of magnitude below NPU prefill rates, because backward passes run on GPU or CPU and weight decompression alone takes 32–42% of backward time [S] ([LCSB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13073)). Zeroth-order training fits in inference memory ([MeZO](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.17333)) but needs 10–100× more steps [S] ([MeBP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03425)), produces gradient estimates at cosine ≈0.001 to true gradients [S] ([MeSP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13069v1)), and nets out 5–70× slower than backprop [calc]. No vendor exposes an NPU backward pass to third parties, no joules-per-training-token figure was found, and Apple trains its developer adapters off-device [S] ([Apple adapters](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter)). The operating envelope has precedent — Gboard trains only while charging, idle and on unmetered networks [S] ([Hard et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1811.03604)), and iOS background processing tasks can require external power [S] ([BGProcessingTask](https://www.andyibanez.com/posts/modern-background-tasks-ios13/)) — and flash endurance is not binding: a 256 GB phone rated at 1,000–3,000 P/E cycles has 64–192 TB of write endurance, so rewriting even 1 GB a day uses 0.2–0.6% of it per year [calc from S] ([Kioxia](https://americas.kioxia.com/content/dam/kioxia/en-us/business/memory/mlc-nand/asset/KIOXIA-TBW-vs-PE-Cycles-Tech-Brief.pdf)).

### Sleep-style consolidation works at small scale and nowhere yet at lifelong scale

Complementary learning systems (CLS) theory proposes a fast hippocampal store for specifics and a slow neocortex that learns through interleaved replay; schema-consistent information integrates quickly, and replaying only similar old items (similarity-weighted interleaved learning, SWIL) speeds integration with less interleaving overall [S] ([McClelland et al. 2020](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/375/1799/20190637/23829/Integration-of-new-information-in-memory-new); [SWIL](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9271163/)). LLM implementations are recent and small. MEGa gives each memory its own gated LoRA and reaches **72.53% on fictional events and 78.03% on 2024 Wikipedia events** against RAG's 82.57% and 88.83%, with "only mild forgetting" over 50 sequential memories and a gate that picks the right memory 85.0–87.8% of the time ([MEGa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21239)). Google's "Language Models Need Sleep" distils fast modules into newly unlocked slower parameters and "dreams" RL-generated rehearsal curricula, reporting 80% on a few-shot ARC subset with Llama-3.2-1B and the best no-context SQuAD results among the compared methods (exact numbers only in an image table); it grows the network, which a fixed phone budget cannot ([LMs Need Sleep](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979)). A CMU/UMD paper with a similar title consolidates context into SSM fast weights rather than permanent memory [abstract] ([Do LMs Need Sleep?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099)), and SCoL's retention reward gives the only measured sleep-like retention gain on a 100-passage stream, reaching 20.46% ([SCoL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07076)). Text-side consolidation warns against letting a model rewrite its own memory: memory banks an LLM continuously updates first help and then degrade, and GPT-5.4 fails 54% of a set of ARC-AGI problems it had solved without memory ([Useful Memories Become Faulty](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.12978)). This project's MNIST experiment ran the full loop at toy scale, where replay generated from a commutative fast memory lifted an ordered-stream backprop learner **from 19.5% to 95.5%** (shuffled reference 98.4%) without storing images ([AGI_BOTTLENECK.md](../AGI_BOTTLENECK.md)). No LLM study measures retention under a sleep schedule over weeks or thousands of facts, and none gives a per-fact replay spacing.

### Every shipped assistant keeps memory outside the weights

Every assistant memory product found lives outside the weights. ChatGPT's saved memories and chat-history reference are injected into context "like a hidden system note" [S] ([OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8983136-what-is-memory)); Claude's memory is topic files the user can read and edit, with no weight changes described ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/claudes-memory-works-everywhere-and-you-decide-whats-in-it)); Gemini's personal context, Apple's on-device semantic index and Meta AI's memory are retrieval or profile stores [S] ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/temporary-chats-privacy-controls/); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/whatis/definition/Apple-Intelligence); [Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/01/building-toward-a-smarter-more-personalized-assistant/)); and Apple states it does not use users' private personal data to train its foundation models [S] ([Apple](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models)). Retrieval memory is strong — Mem0's 2026 algorithm scores **92.5 on LoCoMo and 94.4 on LongMemEval** at about 7K retrieved tokens ([Mem0](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0)) and Zep reports 94.8% on DMR ([Zep](https://huggingface.co/papers/2501.13956)). The only production system found that updates weights from use is Cursor's real-time RL, which retrains Composer about every five hours at population level and raised persisting edits 2.28% while cutting dissatisfied follow-ups 3.13% [S] ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/real-time-rl-for-composer)).

Research prototypes each cover pieces. ALAS searches the web, distils QA and fine-tunes with SFT and DPO, lifting post-cutoff QA **from 15% to 90%**, but it runs on cloud APIs and follows a curriculum rather than user queries ([ALAS](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.15805)); PLUM trains conversations into a per-user LoRA at 81.5%, "competitive with" RAG over 100 conversations ([PLUM](https://huggingface.co/papers/2411.13405)); TMEM distils sessions into fast LoRA weights within a single episode ([TMEM](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.04536)); and Samsung's MemLoRA runs memory operations on-device but stores the content as text ([MemLoRA](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.04763)). Across 14 models, state-of-the-art models and agent memory systems "fail to adapt robustly" to streaming, changing facts ([OAKS](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.07392)), and the notes' component check found no system combining on-device execution, query-triggered web-to-weights learning and user-taught facts in one parametric store.

## The best-supported design splits memory into a fast writer and a slow sparse store

The evidence points to one architecture that meets the brief — frozen base, no text re-read at answer time, and a dedicated set of the model's own parameters changed permanently. It follows the complementary-learning-systems split because every line above shows the same trade-off: writes that are fast (forward-pass writers, closed-form editors, codebooks) do not scale or generalise, and writes that scale and generalise (gradient training on diverse data with replay) are too slow and too token-hungry to run at the moment a user speaks. The design buys immediacy with a fast store and permanence with a slow store, and it keeps the raw episodes so the slow store can be trained, repaired and rebuilt.

### Components and why each was chosen

| Component | What it is | Size (3B-class phone profile) | Why this choice | Main alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen base | 2–4B decoder, 2–4-bit weights | 1.7–2.0 GB at 4-bit [calc] | No drift in base knowledge; keeps hypernetwork and memory keys valid; low-bit is safe because user knowledge lives elsewhere | — |
| Fast store ("day adapter") | D2L-style hypernetwork writes one rank-8 LoRA block per episode on MLP down-projections; blocks concatenated along rank; cleared after consolidation | Hypernetwork 309M params (0.31 GB int8); ≤40 blocks × 5–8 MB fp16 [calc] | Sub-second forward-only write at 82–87% of in-context accuracy; additive, so exactly removable | Larimar matrix (gradient-free, exact deletion, ~512 facts, needs its own architecture); Generative Adapter accumulator |
| Slow store ("cortex") | Product-key memory layer at mid-depth, 2²⁰ slots × 1,024-dim values; keys frozen; values written sparsely | 1.07 GB int8 [calc] | Lowest measured forgetting (SMF); fixed size; small nightly diffs; phone NPUs handle large lookup tables (MeKi) | LocFT-BF localized MLP tuning with replay; NeuralDB gated entries |
| Episode journal | Append-only raw episodes with source, date, stored study items and the slot indices each write touched; never read when answering | ~250 MB/year for a heavy user [calc] | Needed for replay, deletion and base-model upgrades; kept raw because LLM-rewritten memories degrade | Generative replay from the fast store alone (shown only at MNIST scale) |
| Sleep scheduler | Nightly job while charging and idle | — | Gboard and iOS background-task precedent [S] | Mid-day charging windows |
| Guard | Probe set, KL alarm, perplexity check, rollback diffs | ≤0.36 GB for 7 nights [calc] | KL predicts forgetting; single "disabling edits" can wipe memory; eval-gated redeploys work in production [S] | — |

Freezing the base removes one forgetting source outright: base knowledge can be disturbed only by what the memory layer and adapter add at run time, and the adapter is gone by morning. It also keeps the hypernetwork valid, since MEND-style writers drift when "the underlying model continues to evolve" ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)), and keeps the memory layer's keys stable, which matters because representation drift was a separate forgetting source in this project's experiments ([AGI_BOTTLENECK.md](../AGI_BOTTLENECK.md)). Because user knowledge does not live in the base, the base can be quantized aggressively — Apple's roughly 3B on-device model uses 2-bit quantization-aware training ([Apple FM 2025](https://huggingface.co/papers/2507.13575)) — while the writable rows stay at int8, where Physics 3.3 found no capacity loss, rather than int4, which cuts capacity to 0.7 bit/param ([Physics 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)).

The fast store exists because the write has to take effect at the moment of teaching, and nothing gradient-based does that on a phone today: at the measured ~3.8 training tokens/s, even SMF's single 4,096-token step per fact would take about 18 minutes [calc]. D2L writes with one pass through the frozen model plus the hypernetwork — the inference path phone NPUs already accelerate — holds up to about 40 chunk adapters, and answered multi-hop questions over internalized documents at 0.857 of the in-context baseline ([D2L](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.15902)). Because each block is computed from the frozen base and one episode only, the day adapter is a sum of independent deltas, which makes it order-invariant and lets any episode be removed exactly — the commutative-write property that kept this project's counting learners order-invariant ([AGI_BOTTLENECK.md](../AGI_BOTTLENECK.md)). Its price is interference as blocks accumulate (D2L's needle test held to about 40 chunks; Generative Adapter's additive state degrades past 1K–8K tokens), which is why the fast store is emptied every night.

The slow store is a product-key memory layer written by SMF because it holds the best measured forgetting result for a stream of single facts, its size is fixed, and each fact changes only about 500 of 2²⁰ rows (0.05%) [calc], which keeps nightly diffs, rollbacks and deletions small. The strongest alternative, LocFT-BF, has better evidence at scale (100,000 edits under realistic evaluation), but it tunes dense MLP projections and iterates over every stored edit each epoch; for a lifetime of 100,000 facts at 8 study items × 64 tokens each, one epoch is 51M training tokens, about 12 days at 50 tokens/s and about a day even at 600 tokens/s [calc], which rules it out as a nightly routine on a phone. If sparse slots fail at scale, the fallback is NeuralDB's gated in-FFN entries, which reached 100,000 facts with deletion but grow linearly.

The journal is not the retrieval database the brief excludes, because the answering model never reads it. It exists because the evidence says a never-forgetting parametric store needs its training source: LocFT-BF reached 99.8% reliability only by replaying all edits, even 1% replay measurably reduces forgetting ([Ibrahim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763)), and no study shows edits surviving a base-model update or re-quantization. It stays raw and append-only because LLM-rewritten memories first help and then degrade ([Useful Memories Become Faulty](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.12978)), and Mem0's 2026 algorithm likewise moved to "ADD-only… nothing is overwritten" ([Mem0](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0)).

### Teach-once write path: one prefill now, consolidation tonight

The assistant runs as one device-level service, so every chat and window writes to the same memory. An extraction step — the job ChatGPT memory, Mem0 and Samsung's on-device MemLoRA adapters already do ([MemLoRA](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.04763)) — decides that an utterance contains a durable fact and normalises it into a short statement tagged with source and date ("user said, 2026-09-27"). The tag matters twice over: Physics 3.3 found source tags recover most of the capacity lost to noisy data, and "When Personalization Misleads" shows personalized models drifting toward the user's history instead of the truth ([Physics 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405); [Personalization misleads](https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.11000)). The service then asks the current model what it already believes about the subject, and a disagreement flags the episode as a conflict.

The statement, together with a reverse-direction phrasing added because of the reversal curse, passes once through the frozen base; the hypernetwork emits a LoRA block and appends it to the day adapter, at an estimated 0.07 s and under 1 J [calc]. A flagged conflict gets conflict-aware amplitude scaling, since adapters otherwise lose to strong priors and the fix lifts deep-conflict accuracy from 46.4% to 71.0% ([Override Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.23750)). The raw statement, its context, date, source and conflict flag go into the journal. Finally the service asks the model the fact in two or three phrasings with no context and, if recall fails, rewrites the block with extra paraphrases; in Larimar two extra paraphrases lifted paraphrase generalization from 88.4 to 93.6 ([Larimar](https://huggingface.co/papers/2403.11901)). From that moment any later query in any app is answered from weights.

### Search-once write path: answer from the page once, then write it

A query is first answered from the weights, or the model declines. Declining must be calibrated: KBLaM shows a model can learn to refuse when an answer is absent ([KBLaM](https://huggingface.co/papers/2410.10450)), Gekhman et al. show that fitting unknown facts without calibration data raises hallucination [S], and for time-sensitive questions the model compares the newest date tag it can recall with today's date. When it searches, it searches once and reads the results in context to answer now — the only time context is used for that fact. It then extracts the claims the answer rests on, with source and date, cross-checking across sources (a design choice, since ALAS names dependence on source quality as a limitation ([ALAS](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.15805))), writes the page excerpt to the day adapter as a chunk of up to 8K tokens — 0.209–0.551 s for long documents on a GPU, an estimated ~1.5 s for a 2,000-token page on a phone [calc] — and appends the claims and excerpt to the journal. Later queries are answered from the day adapter and, after sleep, from the memory layer; the model searches again only when the stored date is too old for the question or its confidence is low. Stale-fact supersession is built in because tonight's study items for a changed fact include explicit "as of <date>, no longer X; now Y" statements; both EvolvingQA [S] and OAKS show that overwriting outdated facts is where continual learners fail ([OAKS](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.07392)).

### Read path: one forward pass, no text retrieved

Answering is a single forward pass through the frozen base, the memory layer's product-key lookup (top-32 rows for each of 4 heads, about 131 KB of int8 values per token [calc]) and the day adapter, with no text retrieved or injected. The memory lookup adds under 0.01% to the roughly 1.8 GB of weights a 4-bit 3B model reads per token [calc]. A full 40-block day adapter adds about 0.3 GB of reads per token; because decode is bandwidth-bound, that makes decoding up to about 15% slower late in the day, back to baseline after sleep empties it [calc]. For questions that chain facts, the model should first state the relevant facts from its weights and then reason over them: chain-of-thought lifted ROME-edited multi-hop from 7.4% to 19.9% ([MQuAKE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795)), and comparison or classification over stored facts needs chain-of-thought at all ([Physics 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14402)). This uses the model's own recalled text, not retrieval.

### Sleep schedule: generate, distill, replay, gate, commit

| Step | What happens | Evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Snapshot | Record the memory rows about to change | One "disabling edit" can wipe all prior edits ([Gupta et al. 2024](https://huggingface.co/papers/2401.07453)) |
| 1. Generate | ~20 study items per new fact: paraphrases, QA in both directions, implications, links to known entities, negation and fact-check forms, "as of <date>, per <source>" tags, "I don't know" items for adjacent unknowns, superseding statements for changed facts | Physics 3.1; reversal curse; SEAL; Active Reading; Built on Sand [S]; Physics 3.3 source tokens; Gekhman [S] |
| 2. Teacher targets | Base model with the episode in context scores each item (context distillation), so training stays near the model's own distribution | SDFT 89% vs SFT 80%; forgetting ∝ KL shift (RL's Razor) |
| 3. Select slots | Top ≈500 memory rows per fact by TF-IDF or KL, against a background of pretraining usage plus all earlier user writes | SMF; KL selection [S]; including prior user writes is untested |
| 4. Train | SGD on selected rows, mini-batches mixing tonight's facts, scheduled reviews, replay of old facts sharing rows with tonight's writes, and 1–5% general text | LocFT-BF breadth-first batching; 1–5% replay (Ibrahim); SWIL [S] |
| 5. Gate | No-context free-generation recall of new facts and a sample of old ones; general-capability probes; KL on fixed prompts; perplexity | Mirage realistic evaluation; RL's Razor; perplexity as collapse surrogate ([Butterfly](https://huggingface.co/papers/2402.09656)) |
| 6. Commit or roll back | Keep the diff if the gate passes and retire fast blocks whose facts pass from the slow store alone; otherwise roll back and retry with lower learning rate and more replay | Cursor's eval-gated redeploys [S] |

Each new fact is trained on nights 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 with the same 20 items (100 exposures, the 1-bit-per-parameter regime of Physics 3.3 and above the 64 paraphrases per fact SMF used), then reviewed with 8 exposures at nights 32, 64, 128 and onward. The spacing is an assumption that follows from the power-law decay of per-exposure gains ([Chang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11813)); no LLM study gives per-fact spacing parameters. Targeting replay at rows shared with tonight's writes follows SWIL's principle of replaying similar items, and because SMF records exactly which rows each fact touched, the overlap can be computed rather than guessed; whether row overlap tracks semantic overlap well enough is part of experiment 1.

### Forgetting safeguards stack isolation, replay and rollback

No single mechanism in the evidence prevents forgetting, so the design layers them. Isolation comes first — a frozen base, sparse writes to value rows only, and keys that never change — and an IDF background that counts earlier user writes steers new facts away from rows old facts depend on, whereas SMF's published background protects only pretraining usage. On-policy or context-distilled targets keep the KL shift small; replay of row-sharing old facts, spaced reviews and 1–5% general text limit what drift remains; and the nightly gate with rollback catches what gets through. Writable rows stay at int8 or higher. The fraction of rows ever written and the row-sharing rate are monitored, and fresh slots are added as new addresses rather than reshaping old ones: in this project's lifelong run, late capacity growth under stored memories cut the earliest classes' accuracy from 96.0% to 78.8% for a linear readout ([AGI_BOTTLENECK.md](../AGI_BOTTLENECK.md)), and a knowledge-infusion study reports a predictable "collapse point" beyond which retention sharply degrades [abstract] ([Knowledge infusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19371)). When a new base model ships, the memory layer is rebuilt from the journal rather than carried over.

### Deletion is exact in the fast store and repairable in the slow store

When the user says "forget this", the journal entry and its study items are deleted, and the episode's block is removed from the day adapter — exactly, because the adapter is a sum of independent blocks, the same summation property that made deletion in this project's count-based language model identical to retraining with a maximum probability difference of 0.0 ([COUNTING_AI.md](../COUNTING_AI.md)). The slow store cannot delete exactly in that sense, because rows are gradient-trained and shared. Instead, the journal records which rows the fact touched; those rows are restored immediately to their last snapshot from before the fact was first written (about 0.5 MB copied [calc], taking effect at once), and the next sleep re-consolidates the other facts that had written to them. The result is exact with respect to those rows but leaves a second-order residue — the deleted fact was present in forward passes while other rows trained — so it must be verified with probes; Larimar's closed-form deletion, for reference, drove recall of the forgotten fact to 0.0–0.04 ([Larimar](https://huggingface.co/papers/2403.11901)). The same additivity means fast blocks from a user's several devices merge exactly, while consolidated rows do not, so each device would re-consolidate from a merged journal.

### Novelty is limited to the integration

The notes' prior-art check supports only this wording: "To our knowledge, no prior system integrates query-triggered web-to-weights learning and conversation-to-weights learning in one on-device parametric memory with sleep-style replay consolidation." Each component has direct prior art — web lookup to weights (ALAS), self-generated augmentation (SEAL, Self-Tuning, EntiGraph, prompt distillation), sparse low-forgetting post-deployment memory (sparse memory finetuning), sleep and replay consolidation into weights (Behrouz & Mirrokni 2026), conversation to per-user LoRA (PLUM, OPPU, MinT) and agent fast-weight memory (TMEM, Doc-to-LoRA) — so the report claims no novelty for "self-updating LLM", "sleep consolidation for LLMs", "per-user LoRA memory", "sparse memory updates to avoid forgetting" or "learning from web search into weights". The notes found no paper or product measuring, over a longitudinal deployment, how the rate of repeated searches falls as retrieved knowledge is internalized, so a "search once, remember forever" evaluation would be a new measurement. The check did not look for this design's specific mechanisms — an IDF background that includes prior user writes, replay targeted at rows shared with tonight's writes, and deletion by row restore and re-consolidation — so no novelty is claimed for them. The search covered the Hugging Face paper index, the web and GitHub READMEs; it did not cover patents, closed industry work or non-English venues, and arXiv papers from August–September 2026 may be incompletely indexed.

| Tokens per study item | 64 | SMF sequence length |
| Intake | Light 10, heavy 100, extreme 1,000 facts/day; plus 20 web pages/day for a heavy user | Assumption |

The hardware ceiling follows from prefill: 1,770 tokens/s × 2N FLOP per token ≈ 1.06×10¹³ FLOP/s effective. A training token costs about 3N — 2N forward plus about 1N for activation gradients through the upper half of the layers above a mid-depth memory layer, with no weight gradients for frozen weights and no optimizer state under plain SGD — so training could run at about 1,180 tokens/s in bursts and about 600 tokens/s sustained, if vendors exposed an NPU backward pass. None does today.

While answering, RAM holds the base (1.7–2.0 GB), the memory layer (1.07 GB), the day adapter (≤0.3 GB) and roughly 0.3–0.5 GB of KV cache and runtime (assumed), about **3.4–3.9 GB**; a write adds the hypernetwork's 0.31 GB transiently, and sleep needs the base, the memory layer and a training working set under 1 GB (the MeBP class [S]), about **4 GB**. That fits 12–16 GB flagships; on 8 GB phones it works only if the memory layer is offloaded to flash, which has precedent in MeKi's ROM-resident tables ([MeKi](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.03359)).

### Compute and energy per learned fact are dominated by sleep, not by the write

Wake writes are nearly free. A 100-token taught statement costs 100 / 1,770 ≈ 0.056 s of prefill, about 0.07 s with an assumed 30% hypernetwork overhead, and at the 8–10 W device power seen under AI load [S] ([MNN-AECS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19884)) about 0.7 J; a 2,000-token web page costs about 1.5 s and 13 J (0.004 Wh). A heavy user's day of 100 facts and 20 pages costs about 330 J (0.09 Wh), about 0.5% of an 18 Wh battery.

Sleep carries the cost. Over its first ten years each fact needs 6,400 initial training tokens (100 exposures × 64) plus 3,584 review tokens (7 reviews × 8 × 64), about 10,000 training tokens × 9×10⁹ FLOP ≈ **9×10¹³ FLOP**. Study-item generation adds 20 items × 64 = 1,280 decode tokens — 64 s at 20 tokens/s single-stream, or about 10 s if 8 items decode in parallel (assumed, since bandwidth-bound decode lets parallel sequences share weight reads) — and teacher scoring adds 20 × ~200 = 4,000 prefill tokens, about 2.3 s. Energy is time × 5 W.

| Training-throughput tier | Training time per fact | + generation + teacher | Sleep time per fact | Energy per fact | Facts per 8-h night (steady state, with reviews) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measured class, 3.8 tok/s [S] | 10,000 / 3.8 = 2,630 s | +64 +2.3 s | ≈2,700 s (45 min) | 13.5 kJ ≈ **3.7 Wh** | 28,800 / 2,700 ≈ **11** |
| Near-term, 50 tok/s | 200 s | +64 (or +10 batched) +2.3 s | 266 s (212 s batched) | 1.3 kJ ≈ **0.37 Wh** (0.29 Wh batched) | **108** (136 batched) |
| Hardware ceiling, 600 tok/s | 16.7 s | +64 (or +10) +2.3 s | 83 s (29 s batched) | 415 J ≈ 0.12 Wh (0.04 Wh batched) | **347** (≈990 batched) |

The whole night draws 8 h × 3–5 W = **24–40 Wh from the wall**, 1.2–2.6 battery capacities, about **US$0.004–0.006 per night** at US$0.15/kWh. A light user (10 facts/day) therefore fits even the measured tier, a heavy user (100/day) needs the near-term tier, and an extreme user (1,000/day) needs NPU-class training with batched generation. Pushing every fact to the ~1,000-exposure regime that yields 2 bits/param would raise training tokens to about 67,600 per fact and cut near-term throughput to about 20 facts per night — unnecessary, because capacity does not bind. LocFT-BF-style full replay of a 100,000-fact lifetime would take about 12 days per epoch at 50 tokens/s, against the ~3,584 tokens per fact that spaced, row-overlap replay costs; that difference is why the design rests on sparse isolation plus targeted replay.

### Capacity in facts is decades to centuries, depending on intake

The capacity law gives a ceiling for the 1M-slot layer. The law was measured on dense and mixture-of-experts transformers trained from scratch, and Memory Layers at Scale gives a discount for memory parameters: a 1M-key Memory+ layer adds about 2.08B parameters and matches roughly a 2×-dense model, worth about +1.3B dense parameters, so each memory parameter is worth about 0.6 of a dense one, falling below 0.05 at 64M keys ([Memory Layers at Scale](https://huggingface.co/papers/2412.09764)).

## Seven experiments decide whether the design stands

The pass and break thresholds below are design criteria this report proposes, not findings from the notes.

| Priority | Experiment | Setup | Pass / break criterion | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slow-store interference past the overlap point | 1–3B memory-layer model in SMF's configuration; stream 100,000 facts (synthetic personal facts plus WikiBigEdit post-2024 facts) through the full sleep recipe on a GPU. Arms: IDF background with and without prior user writes; replay none / spaced / row-overlap-weighted | Pass: facts 1–1,000 keep ≥90% of their first realistic no-context recall at 100K writes, with held-out NQ F1 drop ≤5%. Break: early-fact recall halves before 10K writes | Whether a fixed-size sparse layer can be the permanent store; if not, switch to NeuralDB-style entries or LocFT-BF with spaced replay |
| 2 | Fast-to-slow hand-off | D2L-style writer for the same base; ≤40 independent episodes per day for 30 simulated days; consolidation teacher = journal-in-context vs fast adapter only | Pass: post-sleep recall within 5 points of same-day recall; 40 independent blocks at ≥90% of single-block recall | Whether the day store handles independent episodes, and whether the journal is needed as teacher |
| 3 | On-phone sleep throughput | Memory-layer backward pass (activation gradients to mid-depth, SGD on ≤500 rows per fact) on a 2025–26 flagship, 8 h while charging | Pass: ≥50 training tokens/s sustained at ≤5 W within skin-temperature limits | Whether the phone serves heavy users (~110–135 facts per night) or only light users (~11) |
| 4 | Search-once longitudinal benchmark | 90-day simulated user with daily news and personal teachings; each question re-asked in fresh sessions at spaced intervals with no context; compared against Mem0-style retrieval and no memory | Pass: repeated-search rate for unchanged facts <5% after the first lookup; no-context accuracy within 10 points of retrieval; stale-answer rate reported | The target behaviour itself, which has never been measured |
| 5 | Use, not just recall | MQuAKE/RippleEdits-style 2–4-hop and reverse queries over taught facts; KID-Bench conflict tiers; negation and fact-check formats; with and without implication items and recall-then-reason prompting | Pass: 2-hop within 10 points of the in-context baseline; strong-prior conflicts ≥70% | Whether stored facts behave as knowledge or only as retrievable strings |
| 6 | Deletion | Delete 1% and 10% of facts by row restore plus re-consolidation; compare with a rebuild from the journal | Pass: deleted-fact recall at never-taught level (Larimar reached 0.0–0.04); retained-fact loss ≤2 points | Whether "forget this" can be honoured without a full rebuild |
| 7 | Lifecycle: base upgrade and precision | Re-consolidate a one-year journal onto a new base; compare int8, fp16 and int4 memory rows under repeated writes | Pass: rebuild within 7 nights; int8 within 2 points of fp16 | Long-term maintainability, and confirmation of the int4 penalty |

Experiment 1 comes first because it tests the assumption everything else depends on: a heavy user crosses SMF's tested range in 10 days and the row-overlap point within three weeks to three and a half months, and if early facts erode there, no engineering elsewhere rescues the design. It is also cheap — 100,000 facts × 10,000 training tokens × 3 × 1.3×10⁹ FLOP ≈ 4×10¹⁸ FLOP, a day or less on one datacenter GPU at an assumed ~10¹⁴ FLOP/s sustained [calc]. Experiment 2 tests the one hand-off with no precedent at all. Experiment 3 is engineering rather than research and can run in parallel with the first two; it decides which user profiles the product can serve, since the gap between the measured ~3.8 and the ceiling ~600 training tokens/s is roughly 150× [calc]. Experiment 4 is the headline behaviour and is meaningful only after the first three pass. Experiment 5 ranks below it deliberately: every line of evidence predicts weak multi-hop use, so it measures how far behind the design is and whether augmentation helps, rather than deciding go or no-go. Experiments 6 and 7 turn a prototype into something that can be kept for years.

## Conclusion

The research moves the question from whether a phone can store a lifetime of personal knowledge in its own weights to whether it can keep it. Storage is settled on paper — the information fits hundreds of times over, and RAM, flash endurance and electricity (under a cent a night) are all small. Three things are open: whether sparse, fixed-size parametric memory keeps early facts intact once writes must share rows, which no study has tested beyond about a thousand facts; whether phone vendors expose NPU backward passes, without which on-device training runs about 150× below what the same silicon achieves for inference-style work; and whether "never forget" can be met by any parametric mechanism alone. The evidence answers the last one no. Every working lifelong result keeps its source examples and replays them, so the realistic form of "never forgets" treats the weights as a compiled, rebuildable form of an append-only record of episodes that is read at night and never at answer time. The brief's no-retrieval-at-answer-time requirement is compatible with that; a requirement to keep no record at all would not be, on current evidence.

The same evidence predicts how a first version would behave. "Remembers what you told it, from any window, without searching again" is within reach of measured components; "reasons with what you told it" is not, because written facts support multi-hop questions at a fraction of the rate of the same facts in context, and corrections to strongly held beliefs land about seven times in ten at best. Skills fall outside this design entirely, since every low-forgetting write method in the notes was tested on facts. The practical order is to prove retention at scale first, measure search-once second, and treat reasoning over learned facts and learned skills as the next research problem rather than a feature of version one.
