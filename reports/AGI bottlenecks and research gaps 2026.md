# AGI Waits on Learning, Not Remembering

As of 30 September 2026, the biggest problem between current AI and AGI is that models cannot turn experience after deployment into durable, general competence. They win olympiads but cannot learn a job. Almost every measurement-based framework and nearly every prominent researcher now names this gap. GPT-5 scored **0 of 10 on long-term memory storage** in the most cited AGI scorecard, and the first continual-learning benchmark found frontier systems capture about **25% of the learnable gain**, with purpose-built memory systems doing worse than plain in-context learning. Every "memory" feature shipped by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and xAI is text put back into the context window. None of their documentation describes post-deployment weight learning. Stated precisely, the unclosed gap is not storing facts; retrieval already does that well. It is learning skills, revising beliefs, and using new knowledge in reasoning as fluently as pretrained knowledge, from few exposures and without forgetting. Against that, this project has built a strong fact store. On GPT-J-6B with all 2,764 MQuAKE edits it answers **95.9%** in the written form and **95.7%** on the dataset's own question, keeps its earliest edits at **97.0%** and leaves **98.7%** of unedited facts unchanged, all scored on freely generated answers. Its combination of exact nightly re-solve, order-independence, exact deletion and a capacity certificate appears new, although each piece has prior art. Functionally, though, it is retrieval by symbolic key, so its fair comparison is with retrieval and graph methods, and there it trails badly. It scores **9.4% multi-hop** against MeLLo's 14.2% (2023) and about 42–49% for 2024–25 methods. Its stored facts are used in reasoning at **0.40** of the model's rate for its own facts, and at **2.5%** when the question is asked directly without a decomposition chain. The direction is worth continuing only under a new research question. First, publish the single-hop result at standard scale with realistic evaluation. Then attack the one gap where parametric memory could beat retrieval: new facts used natively in the model's own reasoning. The pass line is at least 0.8 of the native rate on the audited benchmark (MQuAKE-Remastered) without an external decomposition step, and the line should be abandoned below 0.5. Skills, the core of the AGI bottleneck, are out of reach of this architecture. The "no backward pass, runs on a phone" claims are unproven for open-ended answers.

## Researchers and measurements agree the missing piece is learning after deployment

Evidence labels: unmarked figures were read in primary sources by the research notes. **[S]** marks figures seen only in search snippets or secondary coverage. **[abstract]** marks abstract-only figures. **[calc]** marks arithmetic done in this report. Project figures come from the project's pre-registered runs ([FIXING_THE_BOTTLENECKS.md](../FIXING_THE_BOTTLENECKS.md); [GPT-J summary](../results/reallm4_gptj/summary.md)).

Between April 2025 and August 2026, researchers who disagree about almost everything else named the same missing piece. In October 2025 Karpathy said agents "don't have continual learning. You can't just tell them something and they'll remember it", and put the fix "about a decade" away ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/18/agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)). In November 2025 Sutskever said "these models somehow just generalize dramatically worse than people" and described the target as a continual learner that learns on the job ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/ilya-sutskever-age-of-research-scaling)). Sutton called LLMs a dead end without on-the-job learning ([The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/the-great-ai-debate-are-llms-a-brilliant-leap-or-a-sophisticated-dead-end/)). In February 2026 Hassabis said: "we train them … and then they're kind of frozen and then put out into the world … today's systems don't do that" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/google-deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis-ai-impact-summit-delhi-systems-agi-126021800278_1.html)). Hendrycks and co-authors wrote that most missing capabilities yield to engineering, "but for continual long-term memory storage, we need a breakthrough" [S] ([AI Frontiers](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/agis-last-bottlenecks)). Even Amodei concedes the gap while arguing it does not gate economic impact. In February 2026 he said "even if we never solved continual learning" the impact would be very high, and gave a good chance of solving it within a year or two [S] ([Dwarkesh/YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1E9IZfvGMA)). Among 475 AAAI survey respondents, **76%** judged it unlikely that scaling current approaches reaches AGI ([AAAI](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AAAI-2025-PresPanel-Report-FINAL.pdf)).

Unusually for AGI debates, the measurements say the same thing as the opinions. "A Definition of AGI" scores GPT-4 at 27% and GPT-5 at 57% across ten cognitive domains (drawn from the CHC model of human abilities). **Long-term memory storage is 0 of 10 for both**, and hallucination 0 of 4, and the authors call memory storage "perhaps the most significant bottleneck" ([arXiv 2510.18212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212)). DeepMind's March 2026 cognitive framework says "for many current systems, learning occurs only during training or in-context", and that existing benchmarks are not sufficient to measure learning ([DeepMind](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/measuring-progress-toward-agi/measuring-progress-toward-agi-a-cognitive-framework.pdf)). The most direct test is CL-Bench (June 2026). It covers six expert-validated domains with learnable hidden structure and measures how much better a system gets with accumulated experience than without it. The best system, Claude Sonnet 4.6 with plain full-context in-context learning, captured a **normalized gain of 25.4%**. The dedicated memory system ACE reached 8.6% at the highest cost. The authors found that "naive ICL outperforms systems dedicated to memory management", that frontier systems "are rigid in early, incorrect beliefs", and that "reliable online adaptation remains an open problem" ([arXiv 2606.05661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661)).

From first principles, the reason this gap binds harder than reasoning is simple. A frozen model's competence is fixed when training ends. Every session starts from the same point, and anything learned yesterday must be re-supplied as text. Anthropic's engineering team states it plainly: "Each new session begins with no memory of what came before" ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)). A human employee's error rate falls with tenure because each mistake changes them; a frozen agent repeats the same class of mistake across sessions. That shows up exactly where benchmarks meet real work. On the Remote Labor Index of real freelance projects, the best agent went from 2.5% of deliverables a client would accept (October 2025) to **15.8%** (July 2026) [S], so about 84% of real projects still fail ([arXiv 2510.26787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26787); [CAIS](https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation)). METR's 50% time horizon, the length of task (in expert time) an agent completes half the time, reached about 14.5 hours for Claude Opus 4.6. Reliability is much shorter: an unofficial re-fit of METR's public run data gives early-2026 frontier models 80%-success horizons of only **0.6–1.2 hours** ([METR on X](https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2024923422867030027); [METR data](https://github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public)). The HORIZON study finds that as tasks lengthen, failures shift toward planning errors and forgetting of earlier constraints, and that "scaling base models alone is insufficient" ([arXiv 2604.11978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11978)).

The strongest counter-argument is that context is learning enough. Altman described the ideal as a "tiny reasoning model with a trillion tokens of context" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/sam-altmans-goal-for-chatgpt-to-remember-your-whole-life-is-both-exciting-and-disturbing)). OpenAI's chief scientist appeared on an episode titled "Continual Learning Hype" and favoured large-context learning ([Redpoint](https://www.redpoint.com/content-hub/video/openai-s-chief-scientist-on-continual-learning-hype-rl-beyond-code-and-future-alignment-directions/)). The data partly support this. On ARC-AGI-3, GPT-6 Astra scored **62.7%** when it could carry forward only visible notes, and **99.9%** when OpenAI's harness preserved its reasoning state and compacted the conversation [S] ([ARC Prize on X](https://x.com/arcprize/status/2095597602545025138)). The biggest single lever on a benchmark built to measure learning efficiency was state kept in context, and CL-Bench's winner was plain in-context learning. So the counter-argument is right about adaptation within one episode, and it fails on three counts. Context captured only a quarter of the available gain. Its cost grows with every re-read: the Astra ARC-AGI-3 runs cost roughly $19–26K each [S]. And it does not compound: nothing learned in session 1,000 makes the model generalize better in session 1,001 unless a lab retrains it. No one has published a controlled comparison of weight-level continual learning against context-only memory on the same model, so this remains the field's main untested question.

Sutskever's framing matters because it changes what a fix would be. He treats continual learning as a symptom and poor generalization as the root; if he is right, a memory bolted onto a frozen model treats the symptom. The evidence leans his way on reasoning. RL with verifiable rewards mostly re-weights what the base model can already sample ([arXiv 2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837)). A 100× increase in RL compute moved AIME accuracy only from about 33% to 66% ([Toby Ord](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/how-well-does-rl-scale)). ARC Prize concludes that "AI reasoning capability is tied to LRM knowledge", unlike human reasoning ([arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621)). Several other bottlenecks would survive even a perfect continual learner:

| Bottleneck | Relation to learning after deployment | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Recovering from errors, not repeating them, adapting to a specific job | Mostly downstream | Failures shift to planning and memory as horizons grow ([HORIZON](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11978)); agents "rarely form robust reusable skills" ([SkillEvolBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24117)) |
| Adapting to new environments (ARC-AGI-3) | Mostly downstream, at the within-episode timescale | 62.7% vs 99.9% with preserved state [S] |
| Data wall | Mostly downstream | Deployed learners would generate fresh data ([Era of Experience](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf)) |
| Sample efficiency from sparse rewards | Entangled; the core is independent | Credit assignment remains unsolved ([Ord](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/how-well-does-rl-scale)) |
| Calibration and hallucination | Independent | Evaluations reward guessing ([arXiv 2509.04664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664)); GPT-6 Astra still answers wrongly in 51% of the cases where it is not right [S] ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/benchmarking-gpt-6-astra)) |
| Reward hacking | Independent, probably worse under live learning | Counting reward hacks as successes raises GPT-5.4's METR horizon from 5.7 h to 13 h ([METR on X](https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2042640545126965441)) |
| Physical world models | Mixed | The best interactive world models "only moderate" over 10–60 s ([arXiv 2606.31672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31672)) |

The most defensible single answer is therefore **sample-efficient learning from experience after deployment**, where continual learning and generalization are two views of one missing capability. Reliability, calibration and reward hacking form the next tier and would remain even if it were solved. A September 2026 caveat applies. After GPT-6 Astra, Brockman declared "the AGI era" ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/09/03/openai-greg-brockman-says-its-new-model-astra-is-agi/)), and Chollet moved his forecast earlier while declining to call it AGI [S] ([X/fchollet](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2095598451115614371)). METR's task suite saturates above about 16 hours, so the newest models' long-horizon ability is simply unmeasured. Whether AGI is "here" now depends more on the definition chosen than on any benchmark.

## Frontier labs win olympiads yet still ship memory as text files

What the labs can and cannot do in September 2026 splits cleanly along one line. Problems that fit in one episode with a checkable answer are largely solved. Problems that need accumulated experience or high reliability are not.

| Area | What frontier systems now do | What they still fail | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | Officially graded 42/42 at IMO 2026 (Huawei, Xiaohongshu systems); FrontierMath Tier 4 87.8% in Epoch's own runs [S] | Reasoning is tied to knowledge coverage plus a verifier | [France24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260723-ai-catches-up-with-humans-to-score-100-at-top-maths-contest); [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2); [ARC Prize 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10904) |
| Static novel puzzles | ARC-AGI-2 90.0% (Claude Fable 5.1, $3.12/task) [S] | ARC Prize suspects dense synthetic coverage of the task format | [ARC Prize on X](https://x.com/arcprize/status/2094894027451539744); [arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621) |
| Interactive novel environments | ARC-AGI-3 from <1% (March) to 62.7% (September) [S] | 37-point swing with harness state; about $26K per run [S] | [arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621); [ARC Prize on X](https://x.com/arcprize/status/2095597602545025138) |
| Long tasks | 50% horizon ≥16 h (Mythos Preview, beyond what the suite can measure) | 80% horizon about 1 h in an unofficial re-fit | [METR on X](https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2052896621760004602); [METR data](https://github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public) |
| Real work | GDPval wins or ties with professionals 70.9% (GPT-5.2, OpenAI-reported) | Remote Labor Index 15.8% accepted [S]; OSWorld 2.0 strict 41.7% | [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-2/); [CAIS](https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1) |
| Learning from experience | Better notes, summaries and retrieval | 25.4% of learnable gain; memory systems lose to plain in-context learning | [CL-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661) |

Every shipped memory is external text, and the documentation says so. OpenAI's Codex memory has an offline model rewrite a memory file, then injects at most **2,500 tokens** of summary into the model's instructions ([openai/codex](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/main/codex-rs/memories/README.md)). Claude Code loads "the first 200 lines of MEMORY.md, or the first 25KB", and "Claude treats them as context" ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). Anthropic's Managed Agents "dreaming" reads past sessions and produces "a new, reorganized memory store" of text documents ([Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/dreams)). ChatGPT's June 2026 "Dreaming" synthesizes a per-user memory state "to provide the freshest, most relevant context", and OpenAI reports internal fact recall rising from 41.5% to 82.8% [S] ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-memory-dreaming/)). Google calls Gemini's version "context packing": relevant emails and photos are retrieved into the prompt ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/personal-intelligence/)). Meta AI and Grok expose viewable, deletable memory entries. The only production system found that turns deployment signals into weights is not a frontier lab. Cursor retrains its Composer model from user reactions "as often as every five hours", at population level, and reports agent edits persisting 2.28% more often ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/real-time-rl-for-composer)).

The labs' published research on in-weights learning is small-scale, or it resets. Meta FAIR's sparse memory finetuning is the cleanest result on low-forgetting writes. When learning new facts it cut the held-out NaturalQuestions F1 drop to **11%**, against 71% for LoRA and 89% for full fine-tuning, but only on a 1.3B model, for facts, and up to about a thousand of them ([arXiv 2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103)). Google's Titans, ATLAS and Nested Learning/Hope learn into fast weights at test time. The Nested Learning paper itself says this knowledge "does not persist once the current context is removed" ([arXiv 2512.24695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695)). TTT-E2E compresses a 128K-token context into a 3B model's weights 2.7× faster than full attention, but full attention "dramatically outperforms" it on needle-in-a-haystack recall ([arXiv 2512.23675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675)). DeepSeek's Engram and Qwen's 51B-parameter n-gram tables add large lookup memories, but the authors describe Engram as "static memory" for fixed knowledge learned in pretraining ([arXiv 2601.07372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372); [arXiv 2608.30320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320)). No in-weights continual-learning method paper from OpenAI or Anthropic was found. Anthropic's Sholto Douglas predicted continual learning would be "solved in a satisfying way" in 2026 [S] ([prinz](https://www.prinzai.com/p/predictions-for-2026)), and nothing public shows that it has been. The reported SSI model that learns from experience had not appeared by September ([AIToolsReview](https://aitoolsreview.co.uk/insights/ilya-sutskever-superintelligence-model)).

The labs' choice of text is rational, and the reasons matter for anyone building the alternative. Text memory can be inspected and deleted. It is cheap to serve to millions of users, and it carries over unchanged when a new base model ships, whereas per-user weights have to be rebuilt for each new model. Meta's internal testing flagged memory poisoning ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/meta-personal-ai-agents-public-reckoning-privacy-safety.html)). The price of this choice is visible in the measurements. Google's ReasoningBank paper admits deployed agents "largely fail to learn from their accumulated experience across tasks… doomed to repeat past errors" ([arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25140)). DeepMind's Evo-Memory states that agents "remember what was said but not what was learned" ([arXiv 2511.20857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20857)). PAST-Bench finds gains from retained experience "real but uneven", often not through the intended memory pathway ([arXiv 2608.04003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003)). What the labs cannot yet do is make an agent measurably better at a job with each week on it.

## The open gap is learning skills and using knowledge, not storing facts

Storing and recalling facts is close to solved for practical purposes, and the solution is retrieval. Retrieval beat unsupervised fine-tuning "consistently" for both old and new knowledge ([arXiv 2312.05934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)). In MEGa's head-to-head on Llama-3.1-8B, retrieval scored 82.6% and 88.8% where the best in-weights method scored 72.5% and 78.0% ([arXiv 2504.21239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21239)). Mem0's retrieval memory reports **94.4 on LongMemEval** at about 7K retrieved tokens ([Mem0](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0)). Getting facts into weights works only with heavy augmentation. Single-biography training leaves facts 9.7% extractable against 96.6% with five varied rewrites ([arXiv 2309.14316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316)). EntiGraph needed about 350× synthetic expansion (1.3M to 455M tokens) to lift Llama 3 8B closed-book QuALITY from 39.49% to 56.22% ([arXiv 2409.07431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)). A parametric fact store is therefore not competing with nothing. It competes with a cheap, inspectable, model-agnostic baseline that already works.

The problems nobody has solved lie elsewhere, and the evidence for each is specific:

| Open problem | Best evidence as of September 2026 | Why it stays open | Small-team tractable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning skills, not facts, into weights at more than 10B parameters without replay | Low-forgetting results are fact-only (sparse memory finetuning) or 3–7B skill training (RL's Razor, SDFT); a 0.5B sparse-memory replication gains only 2.5 points on MedMCQA [S] ([arXiv 2605.03229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03229)) | A skill is computation spread across the network, not a lookup | No: needs large-scale RL environments (Anthropic reportedly discussed >$1B on them [S], [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/21/silicon-valley-bets-big-on-environments-to-train-ai-agents/)) |
| Using new knowledge in reasoning (the "knowing–using gap") | Edited GPT-J falls from 40.5% to 5.4% multi-hop at 3,000 edits ([MQuAKE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795)); a 2026 heuristic recovers 58–75% of oracle headroom [abstract] ([Knowing–Using Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08393)) | Written facts sit where the model does not look during internal multi-step inference | **Yes** |
| Overriding strong prior beliefs | Adapter writes fall from 68% on weak priors to 16% on strong ones; best fix 71–72.5% [abstract] ([Override Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.23750)) | A fixed push loses to confidence that grows with familiarity | Yes |
| Consolidating fast test-time memory into permanent weights | "Sleep" papers are 1B-scale ([arXiv 2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979)); Nested Learning memory resets per context ([arXiv 2512.24695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695)) | No proven fast-to-slow transfer | Partly |
| Realistic evaluation | Free-generation evaluation drops single-edit success from 96.8% to 38.5%, and 1,000 sequential edits to about 10% ([Mirage](https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.11177)); 33–76% of MQuAKE labels corrupted ([Remastered](https://github.com/henryzhongsc/MQuAKE-Remastered)) | Teacher-forced scoring and dataset errors inflate results | **Yes** |
| Sample efficiency from few exposures | Every working recipe needs tens to thousands of rewrites per fact; the reversal curse persists without reverse training ([arXiv 2309.12288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12288)) | Facts stored in one direction and format are not extractable in others | Partly |
| Learning on device | About 3.8 training tokens/s measured on phones [S] ([QVAC](https://huggingface.co/blog/qvac/fabric-llm-finetune-bitnet)); no phone chip exposes a backward pass | Hardware accelerates only inference | Engineering, vendor-dependent |

For a small team, one line of this table matters most. The hard core of the AGI bottleneck (skills, generalization, sample-efficient learning from sparse rewards) needs compute and environment budgets that frontier labs spend in the billions. The interface between memory and reasoning is different: whether knowledge written after training takes part in the model's own inference. Data for it exists (MQuAKE-Remastered, WikiBigEdit, RippleEdits), 7–8B models run on a single rented GPU, and the frontier labs are not working on it because their memory is text. Weight editors attack it and fail, and retrieval methods avoid it with explicit decomposition prompts. It is a narrow slice of the bottleneck, the knowledge half and not the skill half, but it is real, measurable and unowned.

## The project built a strong fact store in the wrong comparison class

### Single-hop editing is among the best reported, at an eighth of standard scale

The project's single-hop numbers are genuinely strong under the realistic scoring the field has moved toward: answers are generated freely and string-matched, not teacher-forced. On GPT-J with all 2,764 MQuAKE-CF-3k-v2 edits written 500 per night, the joint memory scores **95.9% on the written form and 95.7% on the dataset question**. The first night's edits hold at **97.0%**, and **98.7%** of 1,071 unedited facts are unchanged. None of the 36 well-known facts whose subjects MQuAKE never edits changed, and each of the 14 unwanted changes is a name collision in the lookup ("Francis" matched inside "Francis II", "Gears of War 3" for "Gears of War 2") ([GPT-J summary](../results/reallm4_gptj/summary.md)). For comparison, the strongest published strict one-edit-per-step result, AlphaEdit with norm anchoring on the full 20,877-edit CounterFact stream, reaches 97.95% efficacy. Its neighbourhood specificity is only **60.44%, against 89.79% for the unedited model**, and those numbers are teacher-forced ([NAS](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.02543)). Under free-generation scoring, published editors fall to about 10% after 1,000 sequential edits ([Mirage](https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.11177)). On the dimensions it measures, the project is at or near the best reported.

Four caveats bound this. First, scale: 2,764 edits is about 7.6× fewer than the standard 20,877-edit CounterFact stream and about 724× fewer than UltraEdit's 2-million-edit claim [calc] ([UltraEdit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.14679)). The joint solve's retention advantage has been shown near capacity only on synthetic facts: 86.8% of the earliest facts at 24,000 facts on a 0.5B model, against 8.4% for nightly batch editing. Second, "question form" is the dataset's own question, which the memory saw when each fact was written. Recall of paraphrases never seen at write time is untested on real edits, except through the multi-hop sub-questions, where it performed poorly. Third, the relation classifier knows a closed set of 36 relations, and subjects are found by matching name spans against stored names. Fourth, the gap to the nightly batch baseline (82.3%) comes mostly from a worse initial fit, not from forgetting (79.3% on night one), because the batch anchor was tuned on the 0.5B model ([FIXING_THE_BOTTLENECKS.md](../FIXING_THE_BOTTLENECKS.md)).

### Measured against retrieval systems, multi-hop trails by four to five times

The project reports that its multi-hop score beats "the published weight editors". That is true, but it is the wrong comparison. MEMIT and MEND write into dense weights and are never told at question time which fact applies. This system finds the subject by string matching, classifies the relation, looks up a stored value and injects it into a hidden state. That is the architecture of retrieval (an entity linker, a relation router and a key-value table) with the retrieved item delivered as a vector instead of text. Its peers are MeLLo, PokeMQA, GMeLLo, KEDKG, CHECK and G-Walk, and against them it trails:

| Method (type) | Model | MQuAKE-CF, all edits | MQuAKE-T, all edits | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MEMIT (dense weight edit) | GPT-J | 5.4% | 0.0% | [MQuAKE v3](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795) |
| MEND (hypernetwork edit) | GPT-J | 6.1% | 4.6% | [MQuAKE v3](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795) |
| **This project (keyed memory + decomposition chain)** | GPT-J | **9.4%** [8.4–10.5] | **18.8%** (750 of 1,868 cases) | [summary](../results/reallm4_gptj/summary.md) |
| Same lookup, fact pasted into each sub-question | GPT-J | 8.4% | – | [summary](../results/reallm4_gptj/summary.md) |
| MeLLo (retrieval + self-check) | GPT-J | 14.2% | 30.7% | [MQuAKE v3](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795) |
| CHECK (retrieval with type checks) | GPT-J | 42.27% [S] | 78.69% [S, attribution conflicting] | [arXiv 2508.00914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00914) |
| GMeLLo (knowledge graph + LLM) | GPT-J | 49.0% [S] | – | [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.844/) |
| G-Walk (graph walk, audited data, 1,000 edits) | 7B | 57.37% [S] | – | [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f782860c2a5d8f675b0066522b8c2cf2-Paper-Conference.pdf) |

GMeLLo and CHECK are about 5.2× and 4.5× ahead [calc]. Dataset versions differ across these papers (original CF-3k, v2, Remastered), so the gaps are indicative, not exact. The project uses v2, which the Remastered audit says still contains 17 duplicates and 633 cases with missing information [S].

Two numbers from the project's own run matter more than the ranking. When the multi-hop question is asked directly, without the external decomposition chain, the memory gives the new answer only **2.5%** of the time, against 1.9% for the unedited model and 2.9% for pasting the fact into the prompt. On its own, the model's internal multi-step reasoning essentially never uses the stored facts. With the chain, the old pre-edit answer appears in 19.5% of cases against 9.4% for the new one, so in about two-thirds of otherwise working chains the memory failed to override the edited step [calc]. Pasting the same matched fact fails in the same way (8.4% new, 19.3% old), which points at the lookup missing the fact when GPT-J words the sub-question itself. The run did not log per-step lookups, so this remains an inference. The use ratio of **0.40** on MQuAKE-CF against **0.82** on MQuAKE-T fits the same explanation: T's two-hop questions end in a head-of-state fact phrased close to the template, while CF edits sit at any of two to four hops across 37 relations, often about an intermediate entity the model names in its own words ([FIXING_THE_BOTTLENECKS.md](../FIXING_THE_BOTTLENECKS.md)).

### The package is new; the pieces are not

The prior-art check supports a narrow novelty claim and rules out a broad one.

| Element | Prior art | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Exact cumulative ridge least squares over all edits | RLSEdit (Jan 2026), on a dense MLP matrix, 10K edits ([arXiv 2601.15686](https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.15686)) | Not new |
| Slots addressed by entity name on a frozen LM, "zero-collateral" edits | Ordo-M (Aug 2026), CounterFact recall 89.0% at the entity's own address [S] ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.ordo-m-sparse-memory-frozen-model)) | Probably not new; details unverified, must be checked before any claim |

Two further caveats. Deleting from the memory restores the base model's behaviour, but it does not erase the base model's original belief, which the frozen weights still hold ([Suppressed, Not Erased](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18985)). And a reviewer will call the design "retrieval with extra steps" unless every result is reported next to a retrieval baseline that uses the same lookup. The project already has one, the pasted-fact arm, and it came within 1 point on multi-hop and 4.9 points on the written form, so the memory's advantage over it rests on single-hop question accuracy (95.7% vs 84.0%) ([summary](../results/reallm4_gptj/summary.md)).

### Three headline goals rest on claims the data do not yet support

"Never forgets" holds only in a narrow sense. The guarantee covers stored constraints below the certified capacity, meaning a stored key maps to a stored target. It does not cover recognition under new wording, or use in reasoning. It also requires an append-only journal of every fact, about 4 KB per fact, which is a database read at night instead of at question time. That design is defensible, but it is not "no database", and it concedes the earlier finding that every working lifelong method keeps its source examples.

"No context window" is misframed. The model still needs a context window to read the question and to reason. What is removed is the context taken up by stored knowledge. That saves money and latency per query, but the project's own GPT-J run shows why it is not an accuracy advantage that will last. Pasting the matched fact scored 10.9% on the question form at 0.5B and 84.0% at 6B, so the gap shrinks as models read context better, and frontier models read context very well.

"Runs on a phone" with "no backward pass" is unproven for the case that matters. The forward-only target (the in-context minus no-context difference) failed at 7.7% recall. The per-answer codebook that worked (96.0%) needs a closed list of answers. In tests 4 and 5 every value was found by Adam steps with a teacher-forced loss through the model's upper blocks on a GPU (`optimize_deltas` in `reallm/memtest4.py`). The nightly sparse solve is plausible on a phone, since bandwidth arithmetic puts it at minutes for 100,000 facts. Writing an open-ended answer still requires a backward pass or a learned target network that has not yet been built.

### Only native use of new facts would justify a parametric memory

Reduced to first principles, a keyed parametric memory with an external lookup does one thing retrieval does not: it delivers the fact as a vector inside the computation instead of as tokens in the prompt. That is worth building only if the vector makes the model treat the fact as its own. The fact would then flow into latent multi-step inference, override the old belief wherever it is used, and compose with other knowledge the way pretrained facts do. If it does not, the memory is a retrieval system that is less inspectable, harder to delete from, and tied to one frozen base model, all the properties the labs avoided by choosing text. The project's own numbers put it on the wrong side of this line today. It reaches 0.40 of native use with a decomposition chain, 2.5% without one, and no multi-hop advantage over pasting the same fact. The 0.82 on MQuAKE-T shows the mechanism can work when the lookup fires, so the question is open and not already answered against the project. It is also the only question whose answer would make this project's direction matter for the AGI bottleneck described above.

## A decisive experiment separates a contribution from a detour

The verdict is to continue, narrowly, with a pre-registered kill line. As an AGI project, a product competing with retrieval memory, or a single-hop editing race, the direction is not worth continuing. The first is structurally out of reach, and the other two are either crowded or already won by cheaper methods. It is worth continuing as a research program on one question: can knowledge written after deployment be used by a model's own reasoning as well as knowledge it learned in pretraining? The team's methodology is an asset here and not a formality. It pre-registers hypotheses, reports failed predictions, reproduces runs digit for digit and diagnoses each failure down to named cases, in a field where 33–76% of a flagship benchmark's labels proved corrupted ([Remastered](https://github.com/henryzhongsc/MQuAKE-Remastered)) and realistic scoring cuts headline editing results by a factor of 2.5 or more ([Mirage](https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.11177)).

Phase 1 should be done whatever happens next. No published method reports all four of efficacy, unseen-paraphrase generalization, near-baseline specificity and early-edit retention under free-generation scoring at around 20,000 sequential edits. NAS comes closest on efficacy but loses about 30 points of specificity. That makes the phase a solid, publishable lifelong-editing paper, and it is honestly framed as a memory result, not an AGI result. Its most likely finding is predictable in advance. Specificity will be excellent because a symbolic lookup does not fire on other subjects, and paraphrase recall will be limited by the relation classifier. Reporting it next to the same-lookup retrieval arm is what makes it credible.

Phase 2 is the actual contribution. The 0.8 threshold is chosen for three reasons. It is above the 58–75% of headroom the 2026 Knowing–Using Gap heuristic recovers [abstract] ([Knowing–Using Gap](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08393)). It is far above dense weight editors, whose ratio is roughly 0.1–0.3 [calc: 5.4–11.8% against 40.5%, prompting not matched] ([MQuAKE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14795)). And it is the level at which "stored facts behave like native facts" becomes a fair description. Requiring no external decomposition is what separates the result from retrieval: MeLLo, GMeLLo and G-Walk all reach their scores by prompting the model through explicit sub-questions over an external store. Arm B is a hypothesis, not a finding. It rests on AcE's observation that implicit subjects act as "query neurons" in multi-hop recall ([AcE](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.07896)), and on the known result that a subject's attributes are recalled at that subject's own token in early-to-middle layers ([ROME](https://huggingface.co/papers/2202.05262)). The project's own test 1 showed that hidden-state keys fail to tell invented people apart. MQuAKE's bridge entities, however, are real entities the model already represents, so the failure need not carry over. If Phase 0's logs show the lookup hits but the value does not take effect, latent addressing will not help, and multi-depth writes become arm B instead. A pass would be the first evidence that facts written after deployment can take part in a model's internal reasoning at the native rate. That is a concrete piece of CL-Bench's "fail to reuse knowledge" problem, and neither the labs (text memory) nor the editors (dense weights) are working toward it. A fail below 0.5 would also be worth publishing, as a clean negative result on whether keyed parametric memory can escape the retrieval comparison.

What the team should not do is equally concrete. Do not extend this architecture to skills: a key-to-vector lookup stores associations, not procedures, and every low-forgetting skill result in the literature comes from RL or self-distillation at scales this team cannot reach ([RL's Razor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259); [SDFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897)). Do not report results only against MEMIT and MEND. Do not keep GPT-J as the headline model beyond comparisons with published tables. Do not describe the system as "never forgets", "no context window" or "AGI" in any external write-up.

## Conclusion

The past eighteen months moved the AGI bottleneck from "can it reason?" to "can it learn from what happens to it?". They also showed that the labs' answer so far is text, rewritten offline and put back into the prompt. That answer is cheap, safe and good enough to reach 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 when state is preserved. It captures only a quarter of the learnable gain on the one benchmark that measures learning directly, and it does not compound. The field's own evidence therefore divides the problem in two. Remembering is largely solved by retrieval. Learning, meaning skills that improve, beliefs that update and new knowledge that joins the model's reasoning, is not. A project that stores facts in weights is valuable exactly to the extent that it moves knowledge from the first category into the second.

For this project, the most informative number is not 95.9%. It is the pair 2.5% (direct questions) and 0.82 (MQuAKE-T with a chain). Together they say that stored facts are inert unless something external fetches them, and nearly native when something does. Whether a model can fetch them itself, through its own internal representations instead of name matching, is a question small enough for a laptop-and-rented-GPU team, large enough to matter, and unanswered by anyone as of today. Answering it, in either direction, is the honest contribution available. Everything else in the current plan either repeats what retrieval already does or reaches for skills this architecture cannot represent.
